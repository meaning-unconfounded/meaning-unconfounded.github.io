4.1 · Three dimensions of every consonant

A core label (B = Boundary) is not enough. Every consonant carries meaning from three layers at once. When all three agree, the operator feels pure:

1 · Identity — what the operator is (B = boundary).

2 · Articulation point — where the body makes it (lips = the outer edge).

3 · Manner — how it is produced (stop = closure and release).

All three say boundary at once for B. That convergence is why B is the purest boundary consonant — and why you can decode words the cards never list. The cards are an instrument. The three dimensions are why the instrument works.

4.3 · Position rule — start, middle, end

An operator is not only what it is. It is where it stands in the word. N is the cleanest example:

Start — negates or initiates (No, Not / Name, Nation)

Middle — continues (Continue, Maintain)

End — arrives or holds presence (Done, Born, Known)

The final consonant often carries the timestamp: D / T seal (arrived, sealed); R still radiates (fire, power); N still continues (known, seen); Y runs without endpoint (energy, journey). SPREAD vs SPRAY is not a one-off — it is a class of distinction. Feel the word as a sequence of operations that begin somewhere and either land or continue.

More position runs

  • T — start: cut opens (TIME). Middle: cut inside (WATER). End: final cut (BIT).
  • S — start: spread announces (SPREAD). Middle: edge inside (LIST). End: residual edge (PASS).
  • B — start: boundary first (BOND). Middle: contained beat (ABOUT). End: sealed stop (GRAB).
id="silent">4.4 · Silent consonants — the exclusion anchor

Only stops and affricates (B, P, T, D, K, G, CH, J) can go silent — because they have a closure phase to withhold. The silent K in KNOW is containment built but never released. The word performs the mechanism it describes.

That is the Exclusion Anchor: meaning present, never voiced. It is also the shape of a hidden postulate in the body — a closure that holds without announcing itself. Examples: KNOW, KNIGHT, KNIFE, KNOCK, SIGN, LAMB, CASTLE, PSYCHE.

This is the bond test at the level of a single word: hear what is being held back, not only what is being said.

4.5 · Verb-to-noun bridge

The verb can hide existence-claims inside actions. “I am depressed” wraps the state as an invisible all-encompassing weather. “The depression in me” makes it a thing — bounded, locatable, addressable. Same operation, discovered twice: in the operator code and in resolution practice (verb-to-noun conversion).

NOUN ≈ N+N — continuation held as continuation: the thing that persists between words.

VERB ≈ V+R+B — bonded radiance bounded: the action that can hide the thing inside motion.

To speak in nouns is to make reality visible. To make reality visible is to let it change.

Composite rule

Read a word by its operators in order. SPREAD = Spread · Project · Radiate · Arrive. SPRAY = Spread · Project · Radiate · Act. The last operator often decides whether the action lands or continues.

When the operator cards feel fluent, the master book’s cluster layer (SP, ST, STR, DR…) opens next — not required here.

More operator runs

FEAR — F (unvoiced friction, edge air) + R (radiate/iterate). Skeleton: sharp edge that keeps spinning. Bond test: is this warning or paralysis?

5 · The four-class know grid

Under every slogan sits a postulate. Under every freeze sits a leg of the cycle.

How the grid uses the operators. Operators tell you what a sound does. The four-class grid tells you what a claim is allowed to do in public speech: must be known, must not be known, must know, must not know. Run a word through operators first; then ask which class the sentence is serving. If the class and the operators fight, rewrite.

Body as quick check (optional, testable). These are not medical claims. They are attention drills:

  • Held breath — continuation refused. Ask: am I blocking N-flow on purpose?

If the drill does nothing for you, skip it. The grid still works on text alone.

6.1 · The three-generation argument

This is not a ranking of peoples. It is a functional progression of what language was asked to do.

Hebrew preserved the consonant root and treated letters as creation-units — the skeleton stayed visible. English scattered the operators across a trading tongue: fast, flexible, opaque. Esperanto optimised for neutrality and ease of learning. The EROS tongue optimises for a fourth property: architectural transparency — you can see the meaning in the skeleton.

Each generation moved toward something. This one names the next move: operators you can feel, vowels that aim them, names a guild can lock without a founder cult.

Compare the four

Hebrew — root preservation high · learning cost high · neutrality low · structural transparency high
English — root preservation low · learning cost medium · neutrality medium · structural transparency low
Esperanto — root preservation medium · learning cost low · neutrality high · structural transparency medium
EROS tongue — root preservation high · learning cost medium · neutrality medium · structural transparency designed high

6.2 · The vowel vectors — the missing half of the code

Consonants carry the primary operator load. Vowels aim them. Change the vowel, change the spatial vector, change the world inside the same skeleton.

A · OpenSingularity / point · unrestricted opening · grounding
E · ExtendExhaust / direction · forward lengthening · bright outward
I · InwardNarrow to a point · line of sight · still, sharp
O · EncompassCircular enclosure · completion loop · containing

U · Rise — Lift / cup from below · vortex · through / transitional.

Test: BIT · BAT · BUT · BOOT · BITE. Skeleton B+T (Boundary + Mark) stays fixed. The vowel shifts the spatial quality of what is bounded.

Prefix shading: in RE- and PRE-, E often acts as directional return rather than pure Up/Out; in DE-, downward. Position layers the root vector — same rule as consonants.

Worked set — BIT / BAT / BUT / BOOT / BITE

  • BIT (I) — inward point; short, sharp, closed path.
  • BAT (A) — open; unrestricted mouth; flat and wide.
  • BUT — central push; neutral rise-and-drop.
  • BOOT (U/O round) — encompass; long held cavity.
  • BITE (I diphthong) — point that opens into travel.

Say them in a row. I narrows, A opens, round vowels fill the mouth. That is the vector system in the body — a contrast you can feel, not a chart you must believe.

id="naming">6.3 · Naming algorithms

Generate in under five minutes. Skeleton first. Vowel second. Bond test last.

Character — (1) Role in one verb. (2) Pick 2–4 operators that encode it. (3) Choose vowel vector (open / extend / inward / encompass / rise). (4) Speak it aloud. (5) Bond test: does it teach or extract?
Example: Teacher-healer → T+H+TH fields → Thoth.

Place — (1) What the place does to those inside it. (2) Operators of enclosure or path. (3) Vowel of containment or arrival. (4) Avoid pure spray endings unless the place is unbound.
Example: Outer shell of the world → R+N+D → Rind.

Faction — (1) Shared hunger. (2) Shared vector (inward integration vs outward disintegration). (3) One distinguishing operator. (4) Name the vector, not the costume.
Example: Inward-forward remnant → fields of continue + boundary → Archive nodes, not race labels.

Artifact — (1) What it measures or binds. (2) Operators of mark / material / bond. (3) Short skeleton (2–3 consonants).
Example: Bond instrument → V field dominant → names that hold vibration, not spectacle.

Spell / operation — (1) Intended state change. (2) Operator sequence as procedure. (3) Final consonant = land or continue (D/T vs Y/R/N).
Example: Seal a truth → end in T/D. Keep a current alive → end in Y/N/R.

Appendix · Glossary

Terms used in the operator method. Keep this near while you work cards.

Minimal pair — two words that differ by one sound and change meaning (SPREAD / SPRAY).

Voiced / unvoiced — voiced: vocal cords vibrate (B, D, G, V). Unvoiced: air only (P, T, K, F).

Fricative — continuous friction (F, V, S, Z, TH). Air never fully stops.

Affricate — stop released into friction (CH, J as in judge).

Approximant — near-vowel glide (W, Y, R, L). Path almost open.

Glottis — the opening at the larynx; H is glottal friction.

Stop (plosive) — full closure then release (P, B, T, D, K, G).

Operator — a consonant treated as an instruction that acts on meaning.
