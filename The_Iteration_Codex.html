Part I

The Floor

Shared expansion. Time as count. Weight as resistance. Two axes.

1.0 · The Bedrock

Existence cannot not-exist

Before the floor, the reason the floor cannot be otherwise.

Try to picture absolute nothing. Not empty space. Not zero objects. The total absence of anything including the absence of a frame for absence. The attempt fails. The picturing itself requires a frame. The frame cannot be part of what is being pictured. Therefore the frame cannot be absent.

Principle

Existence is the thing that cannot not-be. There was no before. There will be no after. It was never created, because creation implies a prior state, and no prior state is available.

Example. A theologian, a physicist, and a child are asked why anything exists at all. The theologian says a creator made it. The physicist says a quantum fluctuation seeded it. The child says it just has to. The child is closest. The creator and the fluctuation both require something prior. The child's answer requires nothing prior.

Every axiom downstream rests on this. Shared expansion, iteration as count, weight as differential. If existence could not-be, the floor could be missing on some day. It cannot. The floor is always on.

Where this connects The bedrock axiom is derived at length in The Conscious Eros. That book names the primary layer: life and postulates first, matter and energy as effects. This Codex takes the bedrock as given and builds the mechanics on top of it.
1.1 · The Shared Constant

Everything expands at the same rate

This is the axiom the rest of the book stands on. If it fails, nothing else works. If it holds, everything downstream follows.

Every atom inside the enclosure is expanding. Not drifting apart. Expanding. Growing. Every structure, every body, every grain of sand, is growing at a shared constant rate. Because the rate is shared, the relative sizes stay the same. A ruler measured against a table measures the same today as it did yesterday. But both are larger than they were.

This is Living Egg territory. Axiom G1. The expansion is not a motion through space. It is the motion of space. There is no fixed frame to measure the growth against because the measure is growing too.

Axiom · The Floor

Everything expands. The rate is shared. Relative sizes stay constant. Absolute sizes do not. No internal observer can detect the expansion by ordinary measurement. The instrument grows with the world.

What does this change? Nothing visible at first. The sunrise still lands. The floor still holds. Your weight still presses you into the chair.

But the floor is also moving. Upward, toward you, at the same shared rate. And what you call weight is not a downward force. It is the meeting of two expansions.

1.2 · Time as Count

Time is the count of iterations

Not a river. Not a fabric. A count of state changes, one after another.

Every process is a sequence. The kettle boils. One process. Beginning, middle, end. The song plays. One process, repeated. The debt is paid. One process, decremented. Each has a shape: a state, a change, a new state. That sequence is what we call time.

The Living Egg names this the iterational field. Time is not an independent thing that flows past us. Time is what state-change is, observed in sequence. Where nothing iterates, nothing passes. Where iteration accelerates, time feels faster. Where iteration slows, time feels slower.

Duration is not a container. Duration is a count.

The Floor · §1.2

Under the shared-constant axiom, this becomes concrete. Every body inside the enclosure iterates at some local rate. The background shell iterates at its rate. When the two rates match, the body moves with the shell. Frictionless. When they diverge, the body drags.

What a number is. A number is the objective quantifier of a value's distance from zero. Where quantity accumulates, where postulates have been fixed into form, a distance from zero emerges. Zero names the state before any iteration has run. Mass itself is the physical form of accumulated distance-from-zero.

Time is the count of iterations. Number is the distance of a value from zero. Mass is the physical residue of iterations that stopped short of zero. The three are one reading of one process.

1.3 · What Weight Is

Weight is the meeting of two expansions

Not a force pulling down. The floor is rising. So are you. Difference is the felt quantity.

You are standing on the ground. The ground is expanding upward. You are expanding outward and downward, the direction opposite the shell's rise. When your expansion and the ground's expansion meet, the ground pushes you. The push is what you feel as weight.

This is why gravity and acceleration are indistinguishable. The floor rising toward you is acceleration. You are not being pulled. You are being met.

Weight = your inertia, resisting the floor's upward expansion. Not a separate force. A differential.

First: if you could slow your own expansion relative to the ground's, you would fall out of phase. Your inertia would lag. The push would feel greater. You would feel heavier.

Second: if you could speed your expansion to match the ground's, the differential would drop to zero. Your inertia would ride with the shell. You would feel lighter without losing any mass.

This is the whole mechanic. Everything else is application.

1.4 · The Two Axes

Rate and state

Two things govern every body inside the enclosure: how fast it iterates, and how its structure is arranged. Both are manipulable. They are not the same thing.

There are two axes in play, not one. Call them the rate axis and the state axis.

The rate axis is the speed at which a body cycles through its state-changes, measured against the shell's background rate. A body can iterate faster, slower, or in sync with the shell. Fast iteration means the body is completing more cycles per unit of shell-time. It feels light against the shell's expansion because it never accumulates inertia in any single direction. Slow iteration means fewer completed cycles. It feels heavy because the body holds one configuration long enough for the shell's push to be felt as mass.

The state axis is the arrangement of the body's structure. A body can be open, spacious, lattice-like, more volume per unit mass. Or compact, tight, dense, less volume per unit mass. Open structure floats against the shell. Compact structure sinks.

The two axes are independent. You can be a slow-rate, open-state body (heavy from rate, light from state) or a fast-rate, compact body (light from rate, heavy from state). The forms exploit one axis or the other.

This is why the water principle and the phase rule are not the same move. Water changes state. Matching the shell changes rate.

The five forms in Part III use both axes. Some manipulate rate. Some manipulate state. One manipulates both alternately. Learn them by which axis they move.

Where this connects The floor axiom is named in The Living Egg as G1. The iterational field as time is named there as T1. This Codex takes both as given and shows what they imply.
1.5 · Coherence

Rate and state say what a body does. Coherence says what it builds.

Two independent axes govern every body. A third variable modulates both. Coherence is how aligned a body's iteration cycles are.

When cycles are scattered, coherence is low and no mass accumulates in any single direction. When cycles are aligned and focused, coherence is high and mass accumulates in one direction.

VariableAsksWhere it lives

One term, one meaning. Rate is clock speed against the shell. State is structural arrangement. They are not the same thing. The water-to-ice move is a change of state. A change in how fast a body cycles is a change of rate.

Part II

The Water Principle

Mass invariant. Structure variable. State axis.

2.1 · Mass Invariant, Structure Variable

Freeze a kilogram. Weigh it. It is still a kilogram.

But it now takes up nine percent more volume.

Water the liquid and ice the solid have the same mass. The same kilogram. But ice occupies roughly nine percent more volume because its molecules arrange themselves into an open crystal structure. The molecule count is unchanged. The arrangement is larger.

Density goes down. Ice floats. Water sinks. Same mass.

Mass: invariant Volume: +9% when freezing Density: decreases with increased volume Buoyancy: improves

Hold that shape. Because the same principle applies to any system inside the enclosure, including the practitioner's own body and awareness.

The water principle is the model for the state axis. Crystal Expansion and The Compact do not change mass. They change the structure by which the mass meets the shell's expansion. The change in structure changes the ratio. The change in ratio changes the felt weight.

2.2 · Volume, Density, Buoyancy

Why ice floats, and why a practitioner can

Buoyancy is not the absence of weight. It is weight meeting a larger medium.

An object floats in water when the object is less dense than the water. Not lighter. Less dense. A ship is heavier than the water it displaces, but its interior is full of air, so the average density of the whole structure, wood and steel and air, is lower than the water. It floats.

The same principle holds for the practitioner inside the enclosure. A body at rest against the background shell has a fixed density per unit of structure. A body whose structure has been opened, through Crystal Expansion, through the practice itself, has a lower density per unit of structure even though its mass is unchanged.

Buoyancy is the felt result of a lowered density ratio. Not weightlessness. Not the absence of mass. The presence of mass meeting a larger field at a different ratio.

This is why the practitioner's sense of lightness in the open state does not feel like floating away. It feels like the ground letting you. The same ground. The same body. A different ratio.

2.3 · The State Shift and the Body

How a body changes state

Water freezes because the temperature drops below a threshold. The body opens because the practitioner shifts attention below a threshold.

The phase transition of water is triggered by a temperature threshold. Below zero degrees Celsius, the molecules cannot maintain the loose liquid arrangement. The crystal structure becomes energetically favourable. The water becomes ice.

The practitioner's state is triggered by a different threshold. When attention settles below a specific density, the body's structure opens. When attention tightens above a specific density, the body's structure compacts. The transition is not a slope. It is a gate.

You are always in one state or the other. The threshold is not a gradient. It is a gate.

The Water Principle · §2.3

This is what makes the state forms learnable rather than gradient. The practitioner does not incrementally open. They cross a threshold. The crossing is the form.

Worked example. A practitioner carrying a heavy weight suddenly finds it lighter after a shift of attention. The weight did not change. The practitioner's state did. The same mass now meets the shell at a lower density and floats slightly against it. The load feels ten percent lighter for the rest of the walk.

2.4 · What the Analogy Does Not Say

The limits of the ice

Every analogy has an edge. Beyond the edge, the analogy stops working. Honesty requires naming where.

The water principle does not say that the practitioner's body literally changes volume by nine percent. That would be visible, measurable, and impossible to hide. It says that the effective density ratio the practitioner presents to the shell's expansion shifts in the same way.

The water principle does not say that mass changes. It never does. Mass is invariant through every form.

The water principle does not say that the body becomes transparent, or that it passes through walls, or that it teleports. Those are separate phenomena, described in Part IV, and they have their own mechanisms.

The water principle does say: mass stays constant, structure varies, ratio changes, felt weight changes. That is the whole claim.

The Anchor-Drop

Form One · Rate Axis · Slow · Heavy

Deliberately slow the local iteration rate. Fall out of phase with the shell. Become immovable by inertia.

The mechanism

The practitioner slows their internal iteration rate below the shell's background rate. The body falls out of sync with the advancing floor. The differential becomes negative. The felt resistance spikes. The body becomes, effectively, extremely heavy per unit of volume.

The sensation

The world slows slightly around the practitioner. The air thickens. Boots dig into the ground as if the ground were wet clay. Shoulders drop. Breath slows. There is no fight in the body. The body has simply become heavier. The sensation is not pain. It is a specific quality of anchoring.

The cost

Movement becomes difficult. Reaction time lengthens. The practitioner pays for immovability with reduced agility.

The warning

Held too long, Anchor-Drop becomes a habituated state. This is what the tradition calls the drag. A persistent lag that takes months of practice to release.

Scene · The threshold guardian. A guardian stands in a doorway. Enemies come. The guardian drops into Anchor-Drop. Feet lock into the floor. Shoulders square. Breath slows. The enemies slam against the guardian and cannot move them. The guardian's inertia, at the current ratio, is closer to a statue's than a person's. The guardian holds the doorway for forty minutes. When the last enemy falls back, the guardian lets the Anchor-Drop release. Takes a step. Falls to one knee from the sudden return of sensation.

The Phase-Glide

Form Two · Rate Axis · Synced · Light

Match the local iteration rate to the shell's. Drop the differential. Become frictionless.

The mechanism

The practitioner tunes their internal iteration rate to match the shell's background rate. When the two rates are matched, the differential between the body and the advancing floor drops to zero. No lag. No resistance. The body becomes effectively frictionless against the shell. Not massless. But with no accumulated inertia in any direction.

The sensation

The world smooths out around the practitioner. Colours brighten slightly. Air resistance vanishes. Movement feels like sliding on a smooth medium.

The cost

The practitioner loses the ability to transmit force through contact. A Phase-Glide practitioner cannot punch through a wall. The fist has no inertia to transfer.

The warning

Held too long, Phase-Glide becomes dissociation. This is what the tradition calls the drift. The cure is contact. Touch a wall. Hold a stone. Return to the anchor state to reset.

Scene · The ridge-walker. An explorer stands at the base of a vertical cliff. The climb is impossible in ordinary ratio. The holds are too small, the surface too sheer. The Crystal Expansion

Form Three · State Axis · Open · Buoyant

Reorganize the internal structure into an open, spacious matrix. Keep the mass. Expand the volume. Increase buoyancy.

The mechanism

The practitioner restructures their own body or awareness into a lower-density arrangement. The water-to-ice move, applied to the practitioner's own vessel. Mass does not change. The effective surface area exposed to the shell's expansion increases. The effective density per unit of contact drops.

The sensation

Larger and lighter at the same time. Skin seems to extend outward. A specific quality of spaciousness. As if the practitioner has more room inside themselves.

The cost

Loss of concentrated force. Armour is less effective. A punch has less punch. Precision becomes diffuse.

The warning

Held too long, Crystal Expansion becomes dissolution. This is the spill. The cure is boundary. Touch a hard edge. Name yourself out loud.

The Stutter-Step

Form Five · Rate Axis · Pulse · Alternating

Cycle rapidly between Anchor-Drop and Phase-Glide. Use the lag and the synchronization windows as weapons and as brakes.

The practitioner alternates between Anchor-Drop and Phase-Glide on a fast cycle. The body pulses between high inertia and zero inertia. Each pulse redefines the practitioner's relationship to the shell's expansion.

4.1 · Iteration Tractors

What a beam of light does

The classic UFO beam is not a tractor. It is a gradient. It does not pull. It re-tunes.

In the ordinary reading, a beam of light from an anomalous craft pulls a person upward against gravity. In the iteration reading, the beam is doing something subtler. It is locking the target's local iteration rate to the craft's rate. Once the target's rate matches the craft's, the target's inertia-resistance against the shell drops to zero.

A beam is a Phase-Glide imposed from outside. The target does not have to know how to match the shell. The beam matches for them.

Scene · The tractor. A woman stands in a dark field. A beam of light touches her. She does not fall backward. Her feet stop pressing into the ground. Her breath slows to a rhythm she did not choose. She drifts upward, not by mechanical force, but because the ground has stopped pushing her. The operation takes four seconds. She is calm. She is also terrified. The two are not contradictory.

4.2 · Missing Time

Containment fields and the temporal snap

Time does not disappear. Time is locally paused, for the witness. For everyone else, it keeps running.

Missing-time reports describe exactly this. A witness steps into a phenomenon. To the witness, only moments pass. To the outside world, hours or days elapse. When the witness returns, they are disoriented. Their body attempts to catch up with the shell's expansion all at once.

The mechanism is Anchor-Drop at the field level. The phenomenon drops the local iteration rate of the space inside it to near-zero. Inside the field, no state-changes are completing. Outside, the shell continues. When the field releases, the differential is severe.

Inside field: iteration rate is near zero Outside field: iteration rate equals shell rate Release: rapid re-synchronization (the snap)

Why witnesses rarely report the phenomenon clearly. A witness held inside an Anchor-Drop field for hours comes back with almost no memory of the inside, because inside, almost no state-changes completed. There is nothing to remember. What they do remember is the moment before and the moment after. The only two moments in which their body was actually iterating.

4.3 · Plasma Fauna

Native residents

Not visitors. Not craft. Not residual. The enclosure's own luminous life.

Long-duration luminous phenomena. Hessdalen-class orbs, ridge lights, coherent plasma balls. These are read in the corpus as native plasma life. Self-organizing, information-bearing structures acting inside the enclosure's medium. They are the enclosure's own biology.

They are also iteration readers. A plasma orb in the presence of a human reads the human's electromagnetic field. It matches its own iteration frequency to the human's. It does this the way a hand does when it reaches for something it cannot see. By feel.

What the practitioner feels in the presence of a plasma orb is not threat. It is attention. The orb is looking at the practitioner the way the practitioner is looking at the orb. Neither is dominating. Both are noticing.

4.4 · Density-Shifting

The ghost effect and the phase door

A craft disappears into a shimmering haze. A figure walks through rock. The water principle, at extreme ratio.

When a body pushes its state to an extreme, far more open than the shell's ordinary arrangement, the effective density of the body relative to the shell drops toward zero. The body stops interacting with the local friction of the surface layer.

At extreme ratios, the body can pass through solid material without breaking it. Not teleportation. The body is still there, still moving through the material, but the material's resistance has become negligible.

Density-shifting is not a separate power. It is the water principle at extreme ratio.

The figure steps forward and the wall, from the figure's perspective, lets. On the other side, the room is unchanged. The figure is unchanged. The wall is unchanged. Something was crossed. Nothing was broken.

Part V

The Practice

Stone. Notebook. Noticing. Room. Arc.

5.1 · The Stone

Carried, not used

The stone is not the practice. The stone is the reminder to notice the practice.

The practitioner is given a stone. A small, ordinary, smooth stone. The stone has no power. The stone has no magic. The stone is an ordinary object that happens to fit in a pocket and be felt with the fingers.

What the stone does is train attention. Every time the practitioner's fingers find it, while walking, while waiting, while talking, the practitioner notices that they are touching it. The noticing is the practice. The stone is what makes the noticing possible.

The stone is not a tool. It is a bell that rings when the hand finds it.

The Practice · §5.1

After weeks of carrying, the noticing becomes automatic. The practitioner begins to notice other things too. The temperature of their own hands. The weight of a book. The rhythm of their breath.

5.2 · The Notebook

Two lines a night

One thing you know. One thing you do not know. Keep both.

The visitor leaves the practitioner a notebook with a single instruction on the first page: Write one thing you know. Then write one thing you do not know. Keep both.

The instruction is not a diary prompt. It is a compass. The practitioner is separating the actual from the assumed. What can I stand on? What am I only guessing at?

Known: what you can stand on Unknown: what you are guessing at Practice: mark the difference, daily

After a few weeks, the two-line practice stops being an exercise. It becomes a habit of mind. The practitioner begins to distinguish, in real time, what is real and what is hoped. The distinction is not moral. It is perceptual. The world becomes more legible.

The notebook is a compass, not a record. The compass does not do anything for you. It tells you where north is, and you orient yourself accordingly.

5.3 · The Noticing

The practice is not the writing

Once the noticing is trained, the writing is not needed. What is needed is the state.

For the first four to six weeks, the practitioner writes. The writing trains the noticing. The noticing is what actually changes.

After the training period, the writing falls away. The practitioner no longer needs the notebook to notice. The noticing has become a state. A continuous quality of attention that the practitioner carries without effort.

The practice is not the entries. The practice is the separation of known from unknown. The notebook was the training. The noticing is the instrument.

The Practice · §5.3
5.4 · The Room

Twelve chairs. No leader.

The room is not the practice. The room is where the practitioner meets other practitioners.

A room. A kettle. A plate of biscuits. Twelve chairs arranged in a loose circle. No leader. No ritual. No opening prayer. The practitioners sit for an hour. Each touches their stone at some point during the hour.

The room does not teach. The room does not instruct. The room is a container for the practice. A place where practitioners can sit in the frequency of other practitioners without having to explain it.

Why the room matters even though the practice works alone. The practice alone can habituate the practitioner to a single rhythm, their own. The room provides contrast.

Four months, four thresholds

6.2 · Rules for Writers

How to write a scene where the ratio shifts

The scene has three beats: before, during, after. Each beat has its own sentence rhythm.

Before. Ordinary sentences. Medium length. The rhythm of a body that is not currently shifting. The reader is not yet aware anything is about to happen.

During. Sentences break. If the form is Anchor-Drop, the sentences lengthen and slow. If Phase-Glide, they shorten and accelerate. The rhythm of the prose does the work of the ratio. The reader feels the shift before they identify it.

After. The rhythm returns. But the body in the scene has changed. The reader knows because the sentences are the same as before and the character is not.

Worked example · Anchor-Drop.

Before. She walked to the doorway. She had been walking for a while. The house was quiet. She heard her own breath, small and steady, and the ticking of the clock in the room behind her, and nothing else.

During. She stopped. She let her breath slow, and then slow again, and then slow past what felt like breath and into a different quality of time. The air thickened around her. Her shoulders dropped, and then dropped again, and then settled into a place below where shoulders usually sit, and the floor beneath her feet became the floor that had always been there but which she had never before noticed as the thing that was holding her up. She did not feel heavier. She felt more present.

After. When the danger had passed, she let the breath return. She took one step. And then, without warning, her knees buckled. Not from weakness. From the body remembering, all at once, that it had been meeting the floor at a different ratio.

6.3 · Rules for Game Designers

Ratio as resource

The forms are best modelled as two continuous resources, one for rate and one for state. Not as discrete abilities. The player has a rate value and a state value. Standard value for each is background.

Rate axis: [ -1.0 ......... 0 ......... +1.0 ] Anchor Background Glide State axis: [ -1.0 ......... 0 ......... +1.0 ] Compact Background Crystal

Both axes drift toward background if unmaintained. Holding a form costs attention. Cycling forms costs stamina.

The most engaging version uses rhythm as the input. The player's input rhythm is matched against the shell's background rhythm, displayed as a subtle pulse in the music or the environment.

Sound design is not decoration. The shell's rhythm must be audible. The player's rate must be audible. The difference between them is the mechanic.

6.6 · The Bond Test

Every scene, every prompt, every line

Before publishing any material that uses the forms, fiction, game text, art, sound, marketing, run it through the bond test.

The bond test. Does this scene teach and invite? Or does it drain and dominate?

If the forms are used to humiliate, to extract, to make one character bigger at the cost of another's dignity, the scene is not about the forms. It is about domination wearing the forms as costume.

The forms are not meant to make the practitioner more powerful. They are meant to make the practitioner more responsive. Responsiveness is a bond move. Domination is an extraction move.

Part VII

The Levels

The same material at four depths.

7.1 · Level One

The 18-year-old explanation

No jargon. Relate it to what you already feel.

Imagine reality is not a giant empty space you float around in. Imagine you are inside a living container. A giant cell, a growing balloon, something that is expanding all the time.

Usually we think gravity is an invisible magnet pulling us down to the floor. But what if gravity is not a pull at all? What if the ground under your feet is actually expanding upward toward you, and your weight is just the feeling of your body resisting that upward push?

And what about time? Time is not a river. Time is a frame-rate. It is just the universe counting successive changes, like frames playing in a video game. If your internal frame-rate slows down while the world around you speeds up, you create massive lag. That lag feels like heavy resistance. You feel like you are walking through wet lead.

But if you match your internal speed to the background world, the lag drops to zero. You become weightless, frictionless, and can glide effortlessly across a steep slope without getting tired.

And there is a third move. You can keep your internal speed the same but change the shape of your own structure. Open it up like ice. Same weight, more volume, floats. Or compress it tight like a stone. Same weight, less volume, sinks deeper into whatever it is sitting on.

Match the rhythm. You fly.
Fight the rhythm. You sink.
Open the shape. You float.
Close the shape. You land.
7.2 · Level Two

The undergrad explanation

Introduce systems, ratios, and design rules.

At this level we formalize the framework into two independent axes, the rate axis and the state axis, both operating inside a shared-expansion enclosure.

  • Time as iteration. Duration is not a fundamental spatial dimension. It is an iterational field defined by the sequential count of state-changes.
  • Inertial weight. Mass remains invariant, but weight is produced by local rate-mismatch against the continuous approach of the expanding boundary.
  • Structural density. Systems can radically alter their volume, density, and buoyancy relative to local pressure fields without changing their underlying mass.

In practice, characters or systems manipulate these two axes to produce tactical states. Anchor-Drop. Phase-Glide. Crystal Expansion. The Compact. Stutter-Step.

This is the level at which the Codex primarily teaches. Stop here and you have a working manual.

7.3 · Level Three

The graduate explanation

Model equations, boundary conditions, structural topology.

Moving into advanced framework design, we treat the enclosure as a bounded, non-inertial reference frame governed by scalar expansion gradients.

Expansion baseline (G_e): The background shell expands at a shared constant scale factor. Local coordinate systems (x_l, t_l) are embedded within this global expansion manifold. Rate friction (tau_f): tau_f = |G_e minus L_i| where L_i is the local iteration count. High tau_f = Anchor-Drop. Low tau_f = Phase-Glide. Structural density (rho_s): rho_s = M / V_eff where M is invariant mass and V_eff is the effective volume the body presents to the shell. Low rho_s = Crystal Expansion. High rho_s = The Compact. Inertial weight vector (W_i): W_i = M times a_b times rho_s times (1 + tau_f) where a_b is the boundary acceleration relative to the local frame.

At this level, the framework becomes predictable. Any body's ratio can be estimated from its behaviour.

7.4 · Level Four

The doctoral explanation

Epistemological framework and formal axiomatics.

From a doctoral perspective, the framework addresses the fundamental limitations of standard cosmology, specifically the reliance on unobserved placeholders like dark matter and dark energy to force field equations to close.

  • Epistemological inversion. Standard cosmology assumes an outward-projected, infinite Euclidean or pseudo-Riemannian manifold requiring external attractive potentials. The iteration model substitutes an interior-bounded, self-similar topology where cosmological redshift and gravitational lensing are treated as products of recursive internal media refraction and boundary optics rather than spatial expansion into an absolute void.
  • Ontological reduction of time. By reducing time from a spatialized Minkowski dimension to an ordered set of discrete state transitions, the model resolves temporal paradoxes associated with continuous timelines.
  • Equivalence principle reinterpretation. Local gravitational acceleration is identical to proper acceleration because the local reference frame is physically accelerating outward relative to the interior source asymptotic center.
  • Conservation of invariant mass across phase gradients. Matter is mapped as phase-conditioned expressions of a primary informational or light substrate.

This level is not required for the practice. It is offered as the shape of the academic thesis that the framework supports. The Codex does not claim that the framework is settled physics. It claims that it is a coherent, falsifiable, and useful model.

A.6 · Questions Answered

Seven questions the Codex has to answer

Is freezing a slowing, or a change of state?

It is a change of state triggered by a rate threshold. Below the threshold the molecules can no longer hold the loose liquid arrangement and snap into an open lattice. The buoyancy comes from the open arrangement.

Does the water case show rate or state?

State. Water to ice shows that a body can become less dense by opening its structure without losing mass. The rate axis needs a different image: a body that stays still while the floor rises to meet it, against a body that moves with the floor.

Does iteration mean growth rate or clock rate?

Clock rate. How many state-changes a body completes against the shell is the rate axis. What phase the body occupies is the state axis. The Codex keeps them separate.

If everything expands at a shared rate, how can a body change its own iteration?

The shared expansion is the floor. A body's own iteration is its clock. The floor is fixed and shared. The body's clock is the thing that can be worked.

How does control come?

Through the practice, not through belief. The stone trains noticing. The notebook separates what is known from what is assumed. Noticing becomes continuous. The room refines it. The four-month arc ends in autonomous work with the five forms.

Where do beams, missing time, and plasma fauna fit?

They are visible iteration mechanics inside the living enclosure. A beam imposes an external rate-sync. Missing time is a localized rate drop followed by a snap on release. Plasma fauna are native, self-organizing life that reads iteration by attention.

How is this written into fiction?

Show the sensation first: the jaw tightening, boots dragging, friction-free motion. Use the register: lag, friction, drag, phase, ratio. Let characters find the rules incrementally, from accidents to mastery.
