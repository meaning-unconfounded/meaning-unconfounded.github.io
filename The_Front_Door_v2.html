Part Five

How the web actually pulls

"Liquidity web" can sound poetic. It describes a mechanism.

The sucking effect

Every EROS pool is paired with another coin (TitanX, DragonX, Hydra, Scale, pDAI, Bear, HEX and 130+ others). When EROS rises, the pool becomes imbalanced and arbitrage bots must acquire the paired coin and deposit it. Multiply by 133: a web of pulls pointing back toward PulseChain, by mathematics rather than hype.

Soft-lock via bridging

Bridging is friction, and friction is commitment: once someone bridges a coin and bonds it into a pool, they are far more likely to leave it there.

The dust-coin revival

Bring the forgotten ecosystem coins (Inferno, Blaze, Helios, JakeX, LegendX, Matrix, Flare, Flux, Felix and the rest) across and bond them into Eros pools, and forty fragmented ghost coins become one coherent map.

Part Thirteen

The chart

Elliott Wave counts are a map some traders use to organise risk, not a guarantee. The community's argument is specific and testable: a nested "1-2, 1-2" structure is widely considered a high-conviction setup because "Wave 3 of 3" compounds several degrees of trend at once.

Applied to EROS: about two years of slow rounding bottom, a stair-step 1-2, 1-2 rhythm, then a tight handle under resistance with higher lows, read by the community as sellers running out of steam where the setup calls for a breakout.

Read this as a thesis, not a guarantee. Wave counts are interpretation, and a textbook setup can still fail. This states the community's technical argument so it can be judged on its merits; it is not a prediction of what the price will do.
