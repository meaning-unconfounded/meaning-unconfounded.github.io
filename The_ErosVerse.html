Who We Are

Identity, Thesis and Motto

One library. A world to enter. A community to build. Inward is forwards. Crypto first. Story second. Extraction never.

Who we are

EROS is a written body of work. It has cosmology, mythology, language, tokenomics, economic architecture, fiction, community philosophy, and immersive-world concepts. It has a website. It has a book library. It has a community that has, over time, produced a substantial archive of messages, video files, audio recordings, voice notes, animations, and stickers — much of it still sitting in Telegram, waiting to be catalogued and used.

EROS is also a proposed economic instrument, with an on-chain token, a set of deployed mechanisms, a set of designed mechanisms, and a set of proposed future mechanisms. The instrument is real enough to be inspected. It is not real enough to be promised. Every technical claim about it in any EROS production must be classified before it is spoken aloud.

EROS is also a community. It has a Telegram archive. It has contributors. It has people who have read the books, people who have not, people who own the token, people who are curious about it, people who want to help build something, and people who want to watch what happens next.

The EROS Series is the project of giving the books a life beyond the page. It is not a single television programme. It is not a single YouTube channel. It is not a single website. It is a connected media universe with several ways into it, and a community production system that lets the universe grow beyond what any central team could produce on its own.

The thesis

Inward is forwards.

This is the philosophical and visual organising principle of the entire project. It is not a slogan chosen for effect. It is the claim the books make: that the interesting direction of inquiry is not outward, into ever-larger structures, but inward, into the enclosure, the boundary, the relationship, the interior of the model, the thing that holds. The Cosmic Egg is an enclosure. The Torus has an inside. The Liquidity Tree has roots. The First Resonance has a room above a bakery. The instrument is a doorway, not the destination.

Every production in the EROS Series should be able to trace its central image or central scene back to this thesis. The cosmology episode is literal. The mythology episode is symbolic. The tokenomics episode is structural. The human story is emotional. But all four are, in their own register, about the inward direction.

The motto

Crypto first. Story second. Extraction never.

This is the order of operations, not the order of importance. The instrument comes first because it is the reason many people will find the project at all. The story comes second because it is the reason they will stay. "Extraction never" is the boundary: the project does not exist to extract value from its audience. It exists to build a world that people want to be part of, and to make participation real.

What we are making

We are making the EROS expression system: a way for the written library to become video, animation, documentary, comics, audio, interactive experiences, and community-created work, under a common identity, with common standards, and with a production pipeline that ordinary contributors can pick up and use.

The expression system has several layers:

  • The books. The 29-file source library. The canon. The thing everything else is downstream of.
  • The series. Fourteen shows, six cycles, one hub. The narrative architecture.
  • The media platforms. YouTube, Shorts, TikTok, Reels, audio, Telegram, the website. The distribution layer.
  • The Guild. Seven production studios. The community engine.
  • The website. The permanent home. The library that never disappears.

The expression system is not any one of these. It is the connection between them.

Why now

The books are written. That is the precondition. You cannot build an expression system for books that do not exist. The books exist. The website exists. The Telegram archive exists. The scripts for the first ten shows exist. The pilot treatments exist. The extraction database exists at version 0.3. The asset register exists at draft 0.1.

What does not exist is the operational document that lets a contributor open a page, understand the whole plan, find a task, complete it, and see it become part of the larger project. This document is that page. It is not the last page. It is the first page of the production era.

Part 1 · The Community Case

Why This Matters

Most tokens launch loud and go quiet. The founders take their fees, their inflation, their exit — and they walk. The tickers are still there. Nobody is home. Value extracts. The community is left holding a chart that no longer means anything.

EROS is built the other way round. The books are already written: cosmology, mythology, language, tokenomics, economic architecture, fiction, community philosophy, immersive-world concepts. The next task is not to write more. It is to give the existing library a life beyond the page — to turn it into a connected publishing, screen, digital-media and community-production system that the community itself can operate.

The central operating idea: one library, many doors, one creative Guild.

The core creative line: "If you do not own it, you're not in it."

The house motto: "Crypto first, story second, extraction never."

The core EROS thesis: "Inward is forwards."

The production system is ambitious in vision and deliberately simple in its first steps. We begin with work that can be made well using available people, tools and source material. Then we expand as capability and participation grow. We do not wait for a central studio. We do not wait for permission. We build the first complete example of the system, then make it easy for others to repeat.

The problem we are solving

A reader who picks up The Living Egg and reads it cover to cover knows what the EROS world model is. A person who has never heard of EROS and sees a twenty-minute video does not. The gap between those two states is the entire job of this project. The books are long. Many people will never read every volume. That is not a failure of the books. It is a fact about audiences.

The mission is to close that gap — to give people who will never read every volume a way in that respects the depth of the source without requiring its length. Through story, through animation, through demonstration, through the use of a visual metaphor that carries the concept. The expression system uses every tool available, not just the one the books already use.

The EROS answer

Build a universe people love because they built it. Not a universe handed down from a central studio. Not a universe that exists to extract value from its audience. A universe that people want to be part of, and that makes participation real. Ownership becomes participation, responsibility, contribution. The world is built by the people who show up to build it, and the people who show up are credited, respected, and able to see their work in the finished product.

The EROS phrase: "If you do not own it, you're not in it."

In this project, ownership also means participation, responsibility and contribution. It does not mean every contributor owns a share of the token. It means the world is built by the people who show up to build it, and the people who show up should be able to point at something and say "I made that."

TitanX on PulseChain — the army is already here

There are ten thousand TitanX holders who forgot they were given a uniform. The bear market took away the noise. It also gave time to build without anyone watching. The question is what happens when people start watching again.

The simplest, highest-leverage action is not creating another token or making enormous promises. It is reminding ten thousand holders what they already hold. The Front Door letter was addressed to the one person who did not walk away — Jake Sharpe built TitanX, and he knows who was here. The four-step sequence in that letter is the first public invitation of the EROS era. The people who stayed when there was nothing to extract are the signal, not the noise. Survival itself is the story.

Ten thousand wallets as a field of dark windows. One lights. Then ten. Then all of them. The price can open the door. Purpose is what makes someone stay.

The burn — how building the universe increases scarcity

EROS is a fixed-supply token on PulseChain. LIVE The supply is 333,333,333,333 — set at birth. The contract has no administrator who can rewrite the rules. Every transfer splits four ways:

🔥

Burn

Part is removed from circulation forever, toward the burn address 0x…0369. The burn address ticks only upward.

🪙

Staking

Part rewards stakers who hold and support the network over time.

🔒

Lock

Part locks staked tokens for a period, deepening commitment.

💧

Liquidity

Part deepens the liquidity pair, making the market more resilient.

Building the universe increases activity. Activity increases burns. Scarcity increases. The value stays in the hands of the people who built it. The design carries its strength and its consequence together. It operates without a discretionary admin, and that cuts both ways. No one can rewrite the rules, and no one can rescue a mistake. Renounced does not mean abandoned. It means the lever is gone. A coin built around a founder expires when the founder leaves. A network compounds.

The two vaults. LIVE One is steel and code — the on-chain vault: contract, rules, transactions, supply mechanics, liquidity arrangements, integrations. The other is a warm library — the cultural vault: books, mythology, art, education, videos, characters, websites, archives, creative assets. The flywheel is the connection between them. If people arrive only for a rising chart, they may leave when it turns red. If they find a universe they value, they have reasons to stay through the quiet seasons.

What this is not

This is not simply a television series. A television series is a single format, a single distribution channel, a single production pipeline. The EROS project is larger than that. It is a connected system that produces a television series as one of its outputs, alongside comics, audio, interactive fiction, tabletop scenarios, zines, short-form video, and community-created work.

We are not trying to turn every book into an episode. The 29-file library is not 29 episodes. It is a source register, and the 14-show slate draws from it selectively. Several books contribute to a single show. One book may support an entire serial. The mapping is by subject and theme, not by file.

We are not asking the community to invent EROS from scratch. The world already exists in the books. The community's job is to interpret, explain, illustrate, test, and build upon what is already there. The Guild provides the organised world. The community gives it living expression.

We are not claiming the EROS cosmology is established science. The EROS world model is a model. The books themselves make this distinction. The expression system must preserve it. The shows can be dramatic, cinematic, and awe-inspiring while being transparent about what is model, what is hypothesis, what is evidence, and what is fiction.

We are not claiming the token mechanisms are all live. Some are deployed. Some are designed. Some are proposed. Every production that discusses a mechanism must classify it before broadcast, and the classification must be checked against current on-chain data at the time of recording.

The EROS role: provide the architecture, standards, direction, initial examples and editorial coordination. We are not the sole producers. We are the team that holds the shape.

The Guild's role: bring the world to life. The Guild makes the diagrams, records the narration, draws the character sheets, animates the transitions, verifies the sources, builds the web components, edits the cutdowns. The Guild is the community production network, organised into seven studios, operating under contribution briefs, submitting work for review, and seeing accepted work enter the production canon.

A contributor in the Sound Studio can be more expert in their domain than anyone on the central team. The structure exists so that expertise can be applied without requiring every contributor to understand every other part of the project. The relationship between the two is not hierarchical in the sense of status. It is hierarchical in the sense of responsibility: EROS holds the standards and the final editorial decisions; the Guild holds the production.

The Plan · §1

1. The Mission

The EROS books have been written. The immediate task is no longer to keep adding material indiscriminately. It is to give the existing body of work a clear path of expression.

We are building a system that can:

  • turn the source library into a connected public experience;
  • translate its strongest ideas into accessible, technically sound and engaging media;
  • provide a repeatable production method for long-form episodes and short-form discovery content;
  • give the EROS community a practical Creative Guild through which people can contribute;
  • keep the source material, editorial decisions, factual claims, scripts, assets and published outputs connected;
  • reduce friction so the community can begin producing useful work without waiting for a large studio or a perfect finished series.

Central operating idea: one library, many doors, one creative Guild.

Core creative line: "If you do not own it / You're not in it."

House motto: "Crypto first, story second, extraction never."

Core EROS thesis: "Inward is forwards."

The production system should be ambitious in vision and deliberately simple in its first steps. We should begin with work that can be made well using available people, tools and source material, then expand as capability and participation grow.

The books exist. The gap is expression.

EROS has a substantial body of written work. The first and most important fact is that the work is done: cosmology, mythology, language, tokenomics, economic architecture, fiction, community philosophy, immersive-world concepts. The books are written. They are not waiting to be written. They are waiting to be expressed.

The gap is not creative. The gap is translational. A reader who picks up The Living Egg and reads it cover to cover knows what the EROS world model is. A person who has never heard of EROS and sees a 20-minute video does not. The translation between those two states is the entire job of this project.

The mission is to close the gap between the people who already understand the books and the people who could understand them if the books were expressed in media they already use. That is the whole mission. Everything else — the 14 shows, the seven studios, the asset register, the evidence system — is a means to that end.

The seven objectives

The Master Project and Guild Blueprint names seven objectives. They are worth stating in full because they are the criteria against which every production decision should be measured.

  1. Make the books accessible to people who will never read every volume. The books are long. Many people will not read them. That is not a failure of the books. It is a fact about audiences. The expression system exists to give those people an entry point that respects the depth of the source while not requiring the length of it.
  2. Communicate difficult ideas through compelling stories, visuals, demonstrations and explanations. The ideas in the EROS library are dense. They can be explained in prose. They can also be explained through story, through animation, through demonstration, through the use of a visual metaphor that carries the concept. The expression system should use every tool available, not just the one the books already use.
  3. Give each book a natural place within a larger EROS universe. A book is not a standalone artefact. It is a contribution to a world. The expression system should show the relationships between books: this book expands that one; this book depends on that one; these two books are alternative treatments of the same idea; this book contradicts that one and here is the log of the contradiction. The universe is the connection, not the collection.
  4. Create a permanent, navigable online home for the material. The website is the home. It is not a marketing site. It is the library. It should be mobile-first, fast, searchable, easy to navigate. It should hold every show page, every source book, every transcript, every diagram, every piece of evidence, every community contribution. It should be the place that never disappears, even if a social platform does.
  5. Use modern distribution platforms to reach new audiences. YouTube, TikTok, Instagram Reels, audio platforms, Telegram, X. These are the discovery layer. They are not the library. The library is the website. The platforms are the roads that lead to it.
  6. Give the EROS community a practical structure for making its own productions. The Guild is that structure. Seven studios. Contribution briefs. A submission process. A canon approval process. The community should not have to invent its own process every time it wants to make something. The Guild provides the process.
  7. Build a growing archive of work that can be reused, expanded and improved. Every production should make the next production easier. The asset library, the character bible, the visual grammar, the episode templates, the prompt library — all of it should accumulate. A contributor should not have to start from zero. They should be able to stand on what has already been built.

What we are not doing

We are not simply making a television series. A television series is a single format, a single distribution channel, a single production pipeline. The EROS project is larger than that. It is a connected system that produces a television series as one of its outputs, alongside comics, audio, interactive fiction, tabletop scenarios, zines, short-form video, and community-created work.

We are not trying to turn every book into an episode. The 29-file library is not 29 episodes. It is a source register, and the 14-show slate draws from it selectively. Several books contribute to a single show. One book may support an entire serial. The mapping is by subject and theme, not by file.

We are not asking the community to invent EROS from scratch. The world already exists in the books. The community's job is to interpret, explain, illustrate, test, and build upon what is already there. The Guild provides the organised world. The community gives it living expression.

We are not claiming the EROS cosmology is established science. The EROS world model is a model. The books themselves make this distinction. The expression system must preserve it. The shows can be dramatic, cinematic, and awe-inspiring while being transparent about what is model, what is hypothesis, what is evidence, and what is fiction.

We are not claiming the token mechanisms are all live. Some are deployed. Some are designed. Some are proposed. Every production that discusses a mechanism must classify it before broadcast, and the classification must be checked against current on-chain data at the time of recording.

Our role and the Guild's role

Our role is to provide the architecture, the standards, the direction, the initial examples, and the editorial coordination. We are not the sole producers. We are the team that holds the shape.

The Guild's role is to bring the world to life. The Guild makes the diagrams, records the narration, draws the character sheets, animates the transitions, verifies the sources, builds the web components, edits the cutdowns. The Guild is the community production network, organised into seven studios, operating under contribution briefs, submitting work for review, and seeing accepted work enter the production canon.

The relationship between the two is not hierarchical in the sense of status. It is hierarchical in the sense of responsibility: we hold the standards and the final editorial decisions; the Guild holds the production. A contributor in the Sound Studio can be more expert in their domain than anyone on the central team. The structure exists so that expertise can be applied without requiring every contributor to understand every other part of the project.

3. Audience Journey & Public-Facing Structure

Do not make newcomers understand the whole EROS universe before they can enter it. Give them a small number of clear doors.

A person can arrive at EROS through any of several doors. They might arrive through a cryptocurrency question — they heard about the token, they looked it up, they found a video, they followed it back. They might arrive through an unusual cosmological idea — they are interested in models of the universe, they found the Living Egg, they started reading. They might arrive through a story — they read the First Resonance, they wanted more. They might arrive through a visual experience — they saw the 3D Universe, they wanted to explore. They might arrive through a community invitation — someone they know said "come help build this."

The job of the expression system is not to funnel everyone through a single entrance. It is to make every entrance a good entrance. Each one should give the visitor a reason to stop, something interesting to understand, something they can check, somewhere to go next, and something useful they can make if they choose to participate.

The four public doors

Instrument

What EROS is as a token/instrument, how it works, and what is documented about its design and operation.

World Model

The EROS cosmology, philosophical framework, operators and models, with clear labels distinguishing model from established science.

Stories

Mythology, fictional narratives, characters, worlds and speculative archives.

Applications

Participation, community, architecture, tools, liquidity concepts and practical projects.

A visitor should be able to start with one question or one short piece and then follow a clear path into a longer episode, source text or practical participation opportunity.

The public journey

The desired audience path is:

Notice →

Served by short-form video, by striking images, by the opening of an episode, by the hook. It asks nothing of the viewer except attention.

Understand →

Served by the flagship episodes, by the explainers, by the illustrated articles. It rewards the viewer with a concept they can now hold.

Explore →

Served by the website, by the library, by the 3D Universe, by the connections between shows. It rewards the viewer who wants to go deeper.

Verify →

Served by the source references, by the evidence labels, by the transcripts, by the primary documents. It rewards the viewer who wants to check.

Participate →

Served by the community, by the Telegram, by the Open Call, by the contribution briefs. It rewards the viewer who wants to do something.

Create →

Served by the Guild, by the studios, by the accepted work and its credit. It rewards the contributor who wants to make something that lasts.

Share

The final stage. Useful work travels outward. The viewer becomes a distributor. The flywheel turns.

The journey is not linear. A person can arrive at Participation without going through Verification. A person can arrive at Creation after a single piece of Curiosity. The stages are not gates. They are stations on a map, and the map should be visible from every station.

The five rules for making content at every stage.

  1. Give people a reason to stop. The first two seconds of a short, the first thirty seconds of an episode, the first paragraph of a page. If the opening does not earn attention, nothing after it matters.
  2. Give them something interesting to understand. The content must teach something. Not a summary of the book, not a list of facts, but a concept the viewer can take away and use.
  3. Give them something they can check. The content must reference its sources. The viewer must be able to follow the trail back to the book, the page, the claim. This is not optional. This is how the project earns trust.
  4. Give them somewhere to go next. Every piece of content should end with a door. The next episode, the related book, the website page, the community discussion. The journey is the point.
  5. Give them something useful they can make. The content should invite participation. Not every viewer will accept, but every viewer should be shown the invitation.

Every published item should make the next step obvious

Avoid forcing every visitor through a long introductory lecture. The public journey is the design principle. Every piece of content is a station on the map, and every station points to the next one. The Map is the entry point. It explains how the cycles connect and lets a viewer choose a direction. The cold-viewer sequence is: Trailer → Show 00: The Map → Show 05: Receipts → viewer choice of world.

Ownership

The EROS phrase is: If you do not own it, you're not in it.

In this project, ownership also means participation, responsibility, and contribution. It does not mean that every contributor owns a share of the token. It means that the world is built by the people who show up to build it, and that the people who show up should be credited, respected, and able to see their work in the finished product.

Ownership is also a design principle for the production system. A contributor should be able to point at something and say "I made that." A contributor should be able to see their name in the credits. A contributor should be able to reuse the assets they and others have made. The system should be built so that contribution is visible, cumulative, and credited.

7. Editorial Integrity & Canon Rules

The project must remain imaginative without confusing fiction, theory, evidence and live mechanisms.

The production system must protect the integrity of the source library while allowing creative expansion. This is the section that defines the boundaries.

7.1 The four-tier canon

Tier 1 — Core Source Canon (Locked)

The 29 foundational source files and verified technical records. These establish what the original corpus says. Immutable reference floor. The 29 HTML texts and the verified on-chain contracts (LIVE).

Tier 2 — Verified Expansion & Consistency-Checked Canon

Scripts, diagrams, adaptations and production material checked against Tier 1. Scripts, cutdowns, and guild productions verified against Tier 1 rules (four operators remain four; no living person named in fiction; every claim tagged).

Tier 3 — Community Variations & Dialects

Independent creative work that honours the core framework but may have its own artistic interpretation. Animation, comics, music, regional nodes like LondonX or BeastX that honour the Egg's core laws and pass the Bond Test.

Tier 4 — Heresies & Experimental Branches

Alternative, divergent, or deliberately speculative work. Allowed to exist openly as labelled alternatives, never silently merged into Tier 1. It may be explored, but it must not be silently merged into the source canon.

The tiers are not a hierarchy of quality. They are a hierarchy of authority. A Tier 4 work can be better than a Tier 2 work and still not be part of the canon.

7.2 The epistemic labels

Every important factual or conceptual claim must be classified. Use explicit labels consistently:

Label Meaning
OBSERVED A measurement, record or event that can be independently checked. Directly documented or independently verified.
MODEL A conceptual or structural model stated by the books. An explanatory framework or interpretation.
HYPOTHESIS A proposed explanation that remains unproven. A proposition that remains to be tested.
PATTERN A proposed relationship or recurring structure, not proof by itself. A comparative interpretation.
OBJECTION A serious challenge or alternative explanation. A substantive challenge.
FALSIFIER What evidence would weaken or disprove the claim. A condition or observation that would weaken or disprove a claim.
STORY Fictional narrative, dramatic device or invented character/event. Fiction, mythology or dramatised narrative.

Use the label that fits the statement. Do not promote a model or story into an observed fact through confident narration. The labels should be visible in the production where they matter. In documentary-style shows, they can be displayed as small on-screen icons. In fiction, they are handled by the fiction contract.

7.3 The mechanism labels

For token and system mechanisms, use a separate system:

Badge Meaning
LIVE Verified as currently deployed and functioning, supported by current on-chain or primary technical evidence. Deployed and verifiable on a PulseChain block explorer today.
DESIGN Specified or designed, but not necessarily deployed. An arrangement of already-live pieces requiring no new smart contract (such as the EROS Liquidity Tree).
PROPOSED An idea or future option, not yet implemented. A design discussion or threshold-gated future specification.

Verification rule. Any claim about contract ownership, total supply, transfer split, burn address, pools, liquidity or deployed token mechanics must be freshly verified before publication. Historical statements in a draft are not sufficient evidence of current state. A mechanism's status must be checked before publication.

Rail A — Documented history and investigation

The Illumination Strategy and Show 08. Primary sources, statutes, public records, dates, citations, verifiable claims, competing explanations and explicit uncertainty. Strictly primary-source, statute-driven, and falsifiable (DOCUMENTED, INFERRED, HYPOTHESIS).

8. Technical & Economic Explanation Rules

The audience should be able to understand the instrument and its architecture without being pushed into hype.

This section is the rulebook for every token-related beat in Shows 05, 06, 07 and 13. It is what separates a documentary from a sales pitch. It is also what protects the project legally and reputationally. Every claim about a mechanism must be checkable before it is spoken aloud.

8.1 The core rules

Explain what is documented

Start with the primary record. If a mechanism is live, show the explorer. If it is designed, show the design. If it is proposed, say so.

Explain what is a design choice

Not every decision is a natural law. Say when a choice was made, by whom, and what alternatives existed.

Explain what remains proposed

A proposed mechanism is a proposal. It is not a product. It may not deploy. It may not be adopted. It is a design discussion, and the discussion is the point.

Explain ownership and participation in plain language

What does holding actually mean? What can a holder do? What can a holder not do? What is the difference between a holder and a builder? Say it plainly.

Explain liquidity as a structure with risks

Not as a guaranteed outcome. Liquidity is a design with dependencies. The dependencies can fail. Say so.

Keep fee and cost discussion brief

Unless a specific technical choice makes a fee material, do not foreground it. Focus on architecture, ownership, liquidity structure, human behaviour and practical design.

8.2 The AMM explanation rule

For automated market makers, the constant-product relationship (x × y = k) can be explained as a model of pool balances. It is a seesaw with two weights. Drop a coin on one side, the other side rises. The larger the coin drop, the more the seesaw tilts.

Shallow liquidity can amplify price movement and slippage. A small trade in a shallow pool produces a large price change. The same trade in a deep pool produces a small price change. This is not a design flaw. It is a feature of the mechanism, and it cuts both ways: shallow pools can amplify selling as easily as buying.

The number of pools alone does not prove depth or usable liquidity. Ten pools of ten dollars each are not the same as one pool of a hundred dollars. The count is not the depth. Depth is what happens when someone tries to trade size through the pool, and the slippage is small.

Do not present the number of liquidity pools as proof of liquidity depth. Do not treat a revoked administrative key as proof that liquidity or market value is protected. Do not present a price increase as guaranteed attention or demand.

8.3 The hybrid NFT and vault rule

Any hybrid NFT, vault, or withdrawal concept needs precise definitions of rights, unlocks, claims, and withdrawal mechanics. Do not imply guaranteed value or liquidity. Locked tokens do not automatically guarantee an NFT's market value or liquidity.

The cultural vault concept refers to the accumulated books, lore, mythology, community archive, art and education. It is distinct from an on-chain vault. Do not merge the two. The cultural vault is a library. The on-chain vault is code and control. The lock is the connection between the market cycle and the cultural cycle, but they are not the same thing.

Do not describe proposed hybrid NFTs, internal economies, or holographic environments as completed products. Keep fictional economic systems distinct from actual financial rights and deployed mechanisms.

8.4 The flywheel rule

The flywheel — attention and price discovery → visibility → discovery of the vault → participation → creation → renewed attention — is a strategic hypothesis, not an automatic mechanism.

The cycle can fail at every step. Attention can fail if the work is not interesting. Discovery can fail if the front door is not clear. Understanding can fail if the explanation is not good. Participation can fail if the ask is too large. Creation can fail if the brief is too vague. Renewed attention can fail if the work does not travel.

The episode should show both sides: the shape of the loop and the place where it can break. Show 06 does this explicitly with the four-frame loop and the red marker that blinks "can fail here" at each frame.

8.5 The verification rule

Any claim about contract ownership, total supply, transfer split, burn address, pools, liquidity or deployed token mechanics must be freshly verified before publication.

Historical statements in a draft are not sufficient evidence of current state. A mechanism's status must be checked against current on-chain data at the time of recording. The date-stamp goes on screen. The check happens again on the day of publication.

The production rule is: if you are going to say "live," show the explorer. If you are going to say "designed," show the design document. If you are going to say "proposed," say "proposed" and mean it.

8.6 What the show must never do

Never Why
Present a price increase as guaranteed attention or demand. Price is an observation, not a mechanism. It can go down as easily as up.
Present the number of liquidity pools as proof of liquidity depth. Count is not depth. Depth is slippage resistance at trade size.
Treat a revoked administrative key as proof that liquidity or market value is protected. Renunciation removes one lever. It does not protect the pool from a large sell.
Describe proposed hybrid NFTs, internal economies, or holographic environments as completed products. Proposed means not deployed. Saying otherwise is dishonest.
Keep fictional economic systems indistinct from actual financial rights and deployed mechanisms. The fiction contract exists for a reason. The audience must be able to tell the difference.
Imply institutional concealment, wrongdoing or scientific proof without robust evidence. Sensationalise the question, not the evidence.

8.7 The Cold-Viewer Trust Test

Every tokenomics episode should pass a simple test: would a sceptical newcomer trust this more after watching it than before?

If the episode is honest about what is live and what is proposed, if it shows its sources on screen, if it names the risk register as a feature rather than hiding it, if it teaches the viewer to check for themselves — then the answer is yes, and the viewer leaves with something they can use.

If the episode hedges, overclaims, hides the risks, or asks for trust without evidence, then the answer is no, and the viewer leaves thinking they have been sold something. That is the failure mode.

The three questions every viewer should be able to ask after watching.

  1. Who can change the rules?
  2. Where is the supply?
  3. Who stays when nothing is pumping?

These are the three questions Show 05 teaches. Ask them of anything. Then see what is left.

10. The Book-to-Media Production Engine

Every source should enter one repeatable pipeline. This is the core operating system of the project.

The pipeline has twelve stages. Every source book, every episode, every short should move through the same sequence. The sequence is what makes the production repeatable. It is what makes each production easier than the last.

10.1 The twelve-stage pipeline

Stage 1 · Ingest

Identify the authoritative file and version. Confirm the canonical filename and source ID. Mark duplicates, overlaps and superseded versions.

Stage 2 · Extract

Capture structure, passages, concepts, claims, characters, visuals and cross-links. The extraction must preserve the author's actual meaning and wording where needed. For every source, capture: canonical title and filename; purpose and subject; main thesis; major sections; key terminology; verbatim passages; characters and narrative material; diagrams and visual concepts; technical mechanisms; evidence and citations; questions and objections; links to other books.

Stage 3 · Classify

Assign evidence labels (OBSERVED, MODEL, HYPOTHESIS, PATTERN, OBJECTION, FALSIFIER, STORY) and mechanism labels (LIVE, DESIGN, PROPOSED). Every substantive claim gets a label. Every mechanism gets a badge.

Stage 4 · Pattern-match

Identify repeated concepts, dependencies, contradictions, missing bridges and unique contributions across books. The production team should compare: repeated concepts; shared terminology; common mechanisms; contradictions; complementary explanations; repeated characters and symbols; similar visual structures; ideas that build upon one another. Each relationship should be recorded as one of: same idea repeated; same idea expanded; complementary idea; dependent idea; contradiction; alternative interpretation; new proposal.

Stage 5 · Editorial synthesis

Decide what the audience needs to understand and what the source can support. Each production receives: one central theme; one intended audience; one learning objective; one opening question; one narrative or explanatory structure; one conclusion; one next step for the viewer.

Stage 6 · Adapt

Produce the episode/short/page/audio brief and script. A book does not automatically become one episode. Several books may combine into one episode, and one book may support an entire serial. The aim is not to compress the books until their technical detail disappears. It is to find the clearest way to communicate the whole idea.

Stage 7 · Research and fact-check

Verify factual, historical, scientific and live technical claims. Every claim that touches the real world goes through a primary source pass. Every mechanism claim goes through a fresh on-chain check at the time of recording.

Stage 8 · Storyboard and asset plan

Specify scenes, diagrams, characters, visual references, sound and required new assets. Separate existing assets from new assets. Mark each asset as READY, REVIEW, EDIT, MISSING or NEW.

Stage 9 · Produce

Record, animate, edit, caption and master. Lock the script before recording. Record the narration as a scratch track and cut an animatic from still boards first to lock pacing before rendering motion.

Stage 10 · Publish

Release the master and derivatives to the right channels. Keep the master on the website. Distribute to YouTube, short-form platforms, audio and Telegram according to the distribution strategy.

Stage 11 · Connect back

Link the published item to its source passages, related shows and next step. Every published piece ends with a door. Every door leads somewhere. The journey is the point.

Stage 12 · Learn and iterate

Use audience questions, retention and community feedback to improve the next release. Review viewer retention, repeat viewers, website visits, book engagement, audience questions, contributor responses, production effort and reusable assets created. Use the results to improve the next production.

12. Parallel Creation & Fractal Branching

The community does not have to wait for the central team to finish a show. They can take a source, concept, character, visual, or task and begin developing something in parallel, while keeping the relationship to the larger EROS project clear.

This is the central community invitation of the project. It is what makes the Guild a production network rather than a fan club. It is what makes the EROS universe expand beyond what any central team could produce. We create. We expect the community to parallel work with us or fractally branch off from our inspiration.

12.1 How it works

1 · Identify

A contributor identifies a piece of the EROS world that interests them — a character, a concept, a source passage, a visual, a question the books leave open.

2 · Check the source

They check the source material. They check the canon tiers. They decide what tier their work will be in.

3 · Propose

They propose a contribution. The proposal is reviewed against the World Bible and the relevant source.

4 · Classify

If accepted, the work enters the production pipeline. If experimental, the work is published as a branch and clearly labelled as such.

5 · Build and submit

The contributor can work alone, or form an independent studio cell with other contributors. The work is produced under the Guild structure and submitted for review.

6 · Publish and credit

The work is published under the EROS banner with the contributor credited. The work enters the archive. It becomes something the next contributor can build on.

12.2 The independent studio cell

A studio cell is a small group — two to five people — who take on a larger contribution together. The cell operates under the same contribution brief format as an individual contributor, but the brief can be larger.

A studio cell can be formed around any part of the EROS universe. A cell might focus on the Gift of Thoth, or on the operators, or on the First Resonance characters, or on the liquidity architecture, or on the 3D Universe, or on a community-created corner of the world that does not yet exist.

The cell does not need central permission to form. It needs to register with the Guild, adopt the contribution brief format, and agree to the canon tiers and the evidence rules. Once registered, the cell can produce work and submit it for review.

The cell's work is published under the EROS banner with the cell credited. The cell can grow, split, or dissolve as its members choose. The point is not to create a permanent organisation. The point is to let people make things together.

Fractal branching. A studio cell can spawn another studio cell. A branch can be forked. A community corner of the universe can become its own sub-universe, still connected to the core, still speaking the same visual language, still honouring the four operators and the Egg's laws. The universe grows by division, not by central expansion. Every branch that passes the Bond Test strengthens the whole.

12.3 The relationship to canon

Parallel work does not automatically become canon. It is classified by tier.

Tier 1 — Core Source Canon

The 29 source files. Locked. A parallel studio cell cannot change Tier 1.

Tier 2 — Verified Expansion

A parallel studio cell's work can be Tier 2 if it is checked against the source and approved.

Tier 3 — Community Variation

It can be Tier 3 if it is a creative interpretation that honours the framework.

Tier 4 — Experimental Branch

It can be Tier 4 if it is deliberately divergent. All three are legitimate. The tier determines how the work relates to the rest of the universe, not whether it is good.

The tiers are not a hierarchy of quality. They are a hierarchy of authority. A Tier 4 work can be better than a Tier 2 work and still not be part of the canon. The question is not "is this good?" The question is "what does this work claim to be, and is that claim accurate?"

12.4 The five contribution pathways

There are several pathways a parallel contributor can take. The pathways are not mutually exclusive. A contributor can start with a single task, move to a personal project, form a studio cell, and eventually produce a branch. The system is designed to let contribution grow.

Pathway What it is Typical commitment
The single task Pick up a Guild brief, complete it, submit it, see it published. The smallest unit of contribution. 1–4 hours
The personal project Take on a larger piece of work — a comic, an audio episode, a visual novel chapter — and produce it under the Guild structure. 1–4 weeks
The studio cell Form a small group to produce a larger work together. Two to five people. Shared brief, shared credit, shared deadline. 1–3 months

12.5 The fractal principle

The EROS universe is designed to grow fractally. Every branch is a complete thing, and every branch contains the seed of the whole. A single door from Sixteen Doors carries the Egg's laws, the four operators, and the bond test in miniature. A single episode of The First Resonance carries the practice, the ledger, and the visitor. A single short from The Web carries the mechanics, the risk, and the philosophy.

This is what makes fractal branching possible. A contributor does not need to understand the whole universe to make something that belongs in it. They need to understand the small piece they are making, and to let that piece carry the shape of the whole.

The fractal test. If you make a door, does the door contain the universe? If you make a scene, does the scene contain the practice? If you make a diagram, does the diagram contain the mechanism? If the answer is yes, the branch is sound. If the answer is no, the branch is decoration.

Contributors can reuse the assets they and others have made. The asset register is open. The visual library is shared.

Ownership that means something

The work is real. The credit is real. The contribution is visible. The contributor can point at something and say "I made that."

A share in the growth

A universe they helped make

The value stays because the community built it. The universe is not extracted from them. It is made by them.

Appendix A · Craft

How to make the thing

A.1 The adaptation thesis

Before writing a single page of any adaptation, write one sentence:

"This adaptation of [BOOK] uses the grammar of [MEDIUM] to make an audience feel [TARGET EXPERIENCE], by centering [CHARACTER / RELATIONSHIP / QUESTION] and letting go of [WHAT YOU ARE DELIBERATELY CUTTING]."

The sentence commits. It names the loss up front, which means it cannot ambush the production in month four.

A.2 The compression math

Conversion What you keep, what you lose
400-page novel → 100–120-page screenplayYou will cut roughly 70% of events, and keep 100% of the emotional arc.
400-page novel → 6-episode audio season (~3.5 hrs)You can keep more plot but must lose all narration and most minor characters.
400-page novel → graphic novel (180–220 pages)Highest fidelity option in visual media, also the slowest and most expensive.
400-page novel → visual novel (8–12 hrs)You can keep almost everything, but must add choice architecture, which means writing new material.
Short story (20 pages) → featureYou must expand 5×. The hardest adaptation there is.

A.3 The unfilmable test

List everything in the book that appears to resist adaptation. Sort each item into one of four buckets:

Cut

It cannot survive. It was load-bearing in prose only. Let it go.

Convert

Same information, new language.

Externalize

Make the interior visible through behavior, object, or another character.

Preserve Elsewhere

It belongs in a different extension of the universe.

A.4 The scene machine

Every scene, in every medium, is built from three components:

Want

Someone wants something right now, in this room. Not their life goal. The thing they want in the next ninety seconds.

Obstacle

Something prevents it. Another person's want, a social rule, their own body, a lie they have already told.

Turn

The scene ends in a different place than it started. If nothing turns, the scene is furniture.

A.5 The subtext ladder

Good dialogue operates at three levels simultaneously:

Text

The literal words. The surface request.

Subtext

What the speaker means and the other character understands.

Metatext

What the scene is really about, which the audience feels but nobody says.

Example: "You should take the umbrella." Text: a practical offer. Subtext: I don't want you to leave, and I can't say that. Metatext: this is the last time I will be able to offer you anything.

A.6 Six ways to adapt interiority

  1. Behavior substitution. Replace the thought with a small, specific action that only makes sense if you know the thought.
  2. Object transference. The character's relationship to an object carries the feeling.
  3. Third-party displacement. The character says the feeling about someone or something else.
  4. Sensory anchor. A recurring sensory detail that the audience learns to read as the interior state.
  5. Structural rhyme. Two scenes in identical staging, with one detail changed, imply the entire journey between them.
  6. Silence. The character does not speak when a lesser character would. The absence is the line.

A.7 Audio drama craft

Audio is the most intimate mass medium ever invented. It arrives in someone's ears, in bed, alone, at 1 a.m. That is exactly the condition under which EROS books are read.

The core rule: every scene must contain at least one silence longer than three seconds. The listener needs those seconds to feel. Take them or the scene will not land.

The format: time-coded scene headings, ambience notes, SFX notes, dialogue with parentheticals that are production instructions, and a silence map.

The design document: every audio episode needs a one-page Audio Design Document with ambience beds, signature SFX, music policy, silence map, and mix notes.

A.8 Comic craft

The gutter — the white space between panels — is the most erotically charged space in any medium. What happens between two panels is imagined by the reader, and imagination is the EROSverse's home territory.

The format: page and panel numbers, shot and angle, content description, dialogue, caption, SFX.

The pacing toolkit: panel size (small = slower, more intimate; large = faster, more spectacular), gutter width (wide = pause; narrow = continuous action), bleed (overwhelm, climax), silent panel (the reader hears the room), page-turn reveal (the single most powerful tool), repeated composition (same shot, changed detail).

The rule: write what is visible. If the script says "she looks sad," the artist will hate you. Write the mouth, the hands, the distance between two bodies, the object in frame.

A.9 Visual novel craft

The core design principle: choices should not change what happens. They should change what it means.

The relationship state model: four variables — Trust, Candor, Desire, Fear. Each scene can move any of them by −2 to +2. Fear is a subtractive variable: players who maximize desire and neglect trust get a scene that is physically intense and emotionally hollow.

The failure mode: the illusion-breaking choice. A choice like "Kiss him / Slap him / Say nothing" where all three lead to the same next line is worse than no choice at all.

A.10 TTRPG craft

The theme-mechanics rule: the rules must mechanically produce the theme. If your game is about the danger of wanting, then wanting must be mechanically dangerous.

Principle Mechanic

A.11 Zine craft

The theme must be a question, not a topic. "What is owed to someone you left?" not "Betrayal."

The ordering is the editing. Arrange pieces so they build an emotional arc across the book. Write the ordering before you solicit the pieces, then reorder as needed.

The pitch call template: theme, length, deadline, canon tier, rights, pay, what we want, what we do not want, pitch format. Never ambiguous. If it is unpaid, say so. If it is paid, state the rate.

A.12 Short-form craft

Three formats that work:

The Exchange

A single line of dialogue, then silence, then a second line that recontextualizes the first. 7–15 seconds.

The Object

One prop from the Sensory Palette, slowly revealed, with a single line of voiceover. 10–20 seconds.

The Confession

A character says the thing they cannot say in the books, to camera, once, and the video ends before we see the reaction. 20–45 seconds.

The failure mode: summarizing the book. The worst possible short-form content is "here's what happens in Book 3." Show one feeling so precisely that people need to know where it came from.

Appendix B · Prompt Patterns for AI-Assisted Production

How to get volume, not summary

Most AI tools summarize because summarization is the safest output. You have to explicitly forbid it and give the model a countable structure.

B.1 The volume contract

"Produce [N] sections. Each section must contain: [exact required element 1], [exact required element 2], [exact required element 3]. Do not summarize. Do not say 'and so on.' Do not use ellipses to skip content. Do not write 'etc.' Every section must be written in full. If you run out of room, stop at a section boundary and say 'CONTINUE' so I can ask for the next batch. Never compress to finish."

The last two sentences convert an unbounded task into a resumable one, which is the single most effective anti-truncation technique.

B.2 Batch then continue

"We are writing a long document. Work in batches of 10 items. For this message, produce Batch 1 (items 1–10) at full length. End your message with the exact line: 'END OF BATCH 1. Reply CONTINUE for items 11–20.' Then stop. Do not begin Batch 2."

B.3 The format lock

"Lock to this exact format for every item. Do not vary it, do not abbreviate it, do not combine items: ## [Number]. [Title]. What it is: [2–4 full sentences]. Why it suits EROS: [2–4 full sentences]. Page budget: [specific numbers]. Structure: [6–10 bullet points, each a full sentence]. Workflow: [numbered steps, 6–8, each a full sentence]. Failure mode: [2–3 full sentences]. Exercise: [3–4 full sentences with constraints]. If a section would be shorter than the minimum, write more — do not move on."

B.4 The forbidden list

"You are forbidden from using any of these words or phrases: 'essentially,' 'in summary,' 'overall,' 'and more,' 'and so on,' 'etc.,' 'various,' 'several,' 'a range of,' 'such as' (as a substitute for listing). If a sentence would require one of these to be shorter, do not shorten it."

B.5 The canon-feed

"Here is the canon you must use. Do not invent facts beyond it. Do not generalize from it. Quote it where relevant. [CANON LEDGER EXCERPT]. Now write [TASK] using only these facts plus what I specify below. If you need a new fact, ask me for it. Do not assume one."

B.6 The draft ladder

"Rung 1: beat sheet only. 20 beats, one full sentence each. No dialogue. Rung 2: scene cards. One card per beat. Who wants what, obstacle, turn. Rung 3: full prose draft of scene [N] only. Minimum 1200 words. Rung 4: dialogue pass on the same scene. Cut 25% of words. Same events. Do Rung 1 only. Stop. Wait for my notes."

B.7 The length audit

"Audit your own output against the contract. For each section, report the sentence count. List every section below the minimum. Then rewrite ONLY those sections, in full, at the required length. Do not rewrite sections that already meet the minimum."

B.8 The voice lock

"Study these three paragraphs from the source book. Identify, in writing: average sentence length, ratio of concrete nouns to abstractions, use of fragments, how interiority is punctuated, the three most frequent sentence openings. Then write [TASK] imitating those measurements exactly. Do not imitate the content. Imitate the measurements."

Present both side by side and let the community decide.

D.4 The relationship between the books and the expression system

The books are the source of truth. The expression system is downstream. But the expression system will, over time, produce new canon — new characters, new scenes, new facts about the world that were not in the books.

Option A

The expression system never produces new canon; it only adapts what exists.

Option B

The expression system can produce new canon, which is then approved by the Canon Council and added to the World Bible.

Option C

The expression system produces new canon only in designated open canon spaces.

Recommendation: the second option. The expression system should be able to produce new canon, because otherwise the universe cannot grow. But the production of new canon should be a deliberate, logged, approved act, not an accident.

What a script is and is not. A written script is not a completed episode. A storyboard is not rendered animation. A proposed asset is not an existing production asset. Every script in this section is a development asset, and the status tables show exactly where each one stands.

Production notes for Show 01

Count the words

The narration runs close to the 2,300 budget. If your edit runs long, cut the Fermi lunch (Act 5) before cutting the objection (Act 6). The objection is what makes the show trustworthy.

Casting

Elara is the only recurring human. One voice actress, or a stylised animated character with no photoreal face. She never speaks to camera.

Chips as design

Chips are a design element, not subtitles. Gold outline, small caps, bottom-left. They appear for three seconds on first use and stay faint for the rest of the scene.

Reuse

The mouth sting, the gold container frame, and the violet myth style become the visual grammar for the whole Cosmology Cycle.

Cutdowns

Act 1 as a 3-minute short ("The Lid"), Act 2 as a 90-second short ("Zero Apples"), Act 4 as a 4-minute short ("The Four"). These feed the top/middle/bottom cinema slots every book carries.

13.1 CORRECTION · the 1962 Atlas 8F claim does not hold as written

What the book said (Illumination Strategy, "1962 · The Tagalongs"): A dummy re-entry vehicle was filmed from a KC-135 aircraft; as it descended at Mach 18, an object appeared alongside it, paced it for ninety seconds and phased out. AARO had deemed the footage a real UAP. The paragraph carried a Layer 1 tag.

What the source pass found:

  • The test was Atlas Missile 8F, launched 19 September 1962 at 14:30 EST from Complex 11, Atlantic Missile Range, range test number 103.
  • The flight test report is public on DTIC as AD0861789 ("Flight Test Report, Atlas Missile 8F", dated 2 October 1962, declassified from Secret).
  • The National Archives holds a film of the flight, catalogued as NAID 614788, titled "Unidentified Flying Object (UFO) Sighting" and described as aerial coverage of a flight of Atlas F (Test 103).
  • The sentence the book quotes is real and is on page 14. But it sits inside the section on the staging cameras, which filmed the booster section being jettisoned at booster cutoff, roughly two minutes into the flight. Camera No. 3 was mounted outside the missile facing aft and ran from about booster cutoff plus 0.5 seconds to plus 10 seconds.

What this means: The "paced the re-entry vehicle for ninety seconds" description is not what the report says. It is about debris around a separating booster stage, in a ten-second window, about half an hour before the re-entry vehicle came down. The fix is a stronger scene, not a weaker one: it dramatises the method the show is about.

The fix, for the book and for the show.

Production notes and limits

These are the genuine technical limits, not caveats.

  • Shot length. Current AI video tools produce short clips, a few seconds each. A twenty-minute episode is a few hundred shots. Every board in this document is written as a single prompt unit, so the storyboard is the shot list.
  • Character drift. Faces and costumes drift between clips. Lock Elara and the narrator's avatar with a character sheet before generating anything. The myth sequences are safer because the figures are abstract.
  • Text in video. Generated text is unreliable. Every chip, contract address, equation and caption is composited in the editor, never generated.
  • Voice. Record the narrator once, as a scratch track, and cut an animatic from stills first. Cutting the script against a voice is where the 2,300-word budget actually gets tested.
  • NotebookLM. Feeding a script into NotebookLM as a source gives you a style-consistent rough cut and a mind map for free. It will not hold the three-register look, so treat it as a rough cut, not a master.

Part 6 · The Invitation

The Invitation

This is the section that answers the only question that matters: what can I do?

You do not have to wait for a central team to finish a show. You can take a source, a concept, a character, a visual, or a task and begin developing something in parallel, while keeping the relationship to the larger EROS project clear. That is the central community invitation of the project. It is what makes the Guild a production network rather than a fan club.

We create. We expect the community to parallel work with us or fractally branch off from our inspiration.

Five Contribution Pathways

There are several pathways a parallel contributor can take. The pathways are not mutually exclusive. You can start with a single task, move to a personal project, form a studio cell, and eventually produce a branch. The system is designed to let contribution grow.

PATHWAY 1

The Single Task

Pick up a Guild brief, complete it, submit it, see it published. The smallest unit of contribution. You do not need to understand the whole universe to make one thing that belongs in it.

1–4 HOURS

PATHWAY 2

The Personal Project

Take on a larger piece of work — a comic, an audio episode, a visual novel chapter — and produce it under the Guild structure. One person, one larger deliverable, the same brief format and the same review process.

1–4 WEEKS

PATHWAY 3

The Studio Cell

Form a small group to produce a larger work together. Two to five people. Shared brief, shared credit, shared deadline. A cell can grow, split, or dissolve as its members choose. It does not need central permission to form. It needs to register, adopt the brief format, and agree to the canon tiers.

1–3 MONTHS

PATHWAY 4

The Experimental Branch

Produce work that deliberately diverges from the canon, and publish it as a Tier 4 branch. Labelled clearly. Allowed to exist openly. The universe grows by division, not by central expansion. Every branch that passes the Bond Test strengthens the whole.

VARIABLE

PATHWAY 5

The Research Contribution

Verify sources, build evidence registers, check claims. Research is a contribution pathway equal to production. The Atlas 8F correction in Show 08 is proof that a single verification pass can turn an overclaim into the strongest scene in the episode. Research is not a supporting role. It is the role that keeps the whole project credible.

1–8 HOURS PER CLAIM

The Four-Tier Canon

Parallel work does not automatically become canon. It is classified by tier. The tiers are not a hierarchy of quality. They are a hierarchy of authority. A Tier 4 work can be better than a Tier 2 work and still not be part of the canon.

Tier 1 · Core Source Canon

The 29 foundational source files and verified technical records. Locked. Immutable reference floor. The canon. The thing everything else is downstream of.

Tier 2 · Verified Expansion

Scripts, diagrams, adaptations and production material checked against Tier 1. A parallel studio cell's work can be Tier 2 if it is checked against the source and approved.

Tier 3 · Community Variation

Independent creative work that honours the core framework but may have its own artistic interpretation. It can be Tier 3 if it is a creative interpretation that honours the framework and passes the Bond Test.

Tier 4 · Experimental Branch

Alternative, divergent, or deliberately speculative work. Allowed to exist openly as a labelled alternative, never silently merged into the canon.

The fractal test. If you make a door, does the door contain the universe? If you make a scene, does the scene contain the practice? If you make a diagram, does the diagram contain the mechanism? If the answer is yes, the branch is sound. If the answer is no, the branch is decoration.
