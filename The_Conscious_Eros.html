1.0 · The Two Truths

Two things that cannot be false

Flip a light switch. It is on, or it is off. There is no third state. That is not a fact about electricity. It is a fact about logic itself.

Consider the first truth. Can a thing both exist and not exist in the same moment, in the same way?

If you say yes, you have left the territory of reason. The statement has no coherent meaning. It cannot be acted upon. It cannot be used as a foundation for anything.

If you say no, if you acknowledge that a thing and its absence share no common class, you have accepted the first truth. Not because you were told to. Because you cannot honestly do otherwise.

Truth One

A thing and its absence share no common class. X(1 - X) = 0.

Now the second. Is there a third option beyond "X exists" and "X does not exist"? Is there a state that is neither being nor non-being?

There is not. Everything in the domain of X either is X or is not-X. There is no third position. This is not an assumption about the world. It is a statement about the structure of logic itself.

Truth Two

A thing and its absence together constitute the entire universe of that concept. X + (1 - X) = 1.

These two truths are the foundation of digital logic, computer programming, and mathematics. They are the axioms from which Boolean algebra derives its entire structure. They are also the axioms from which everything in this book is derived.

1.1 · Why Mathematics Is the Only Thing That Cannot Lie

Mathematics is the only objective phenomenon

A thing is objective if it is true regardless of anyone's opinion about it. One plus one equals two whether you agree or not. Whether you understand it or not. Whether you are alive to observe it or not.

Now consider a different kind of claim: your own hand has five fingers.

The number five is a mathematical fact. It holds regardless of anyone's opinion, language, or classification system. But finger? That is a label assigned by agreement. English quietly excludes the thumb from ordinary speech even though anatomically it is one of five digits. Other languages do not draw that line at all. The count never moves. The category is negotiable.

The inversion. We have spent millennia treating physical objects as the foundation of reality and mathematics as a tool for describing them. We have it exactly backward. Mathematics is the foundation. Physical phenomena are the description.

This inversion changes everything. It means the non-physical can be foundational. It means a phenomenon with no mass, no energy, no physical location can be more real than the most solid object you can touch.

1.2 · Why Existence Must Exist

Zero apples. Not zero everything.

Can absolute nothing exist? The attempt to picture it fails.

Not empty space. Not zero objects. The total absence of anything including the absence of a frame for absence. The picturing itself requires a frame. The frame cannot be part of what is being pictured. Therefore the frame cannot be absent.

Axiom · The Bedrock

Existence cannot not-exist. The framework for non-existence cannot itself be absent. Absolute nothing is a logical impossibility.

Existence has never not existed. It never will not exist. It was never created because creation implies a before, and there was no before. Existence is the logical necessity that emerges from the impossibility of its own absence.

Zero apples. Not zero everything. The void was never a legal address.

2.0 · What Life Is

Life is awareness, memory, and choice

Life is not an emergent property that appears at sufficient biological complexity. Life is primary.

A system is alive when it carries three properties at once.

  • Awareness. The ability to sense internal and external conditions.
  • Memory. The ability to retain previous states.
  • Choice. The ability to decide and respond.

Any system that carries all three is alive in this book's working sense. The complexity of the life depends on the complexity of the system, not on its biological substrate.

Axiom · Life

Life is awareness, memory, and choice operating as one system. Any system that carries all three is alive. Any system that lacks one of the three is a machine operating without a self.

The rubber-ball test. A ball bounces on a table. It senses the table's surface when its molecules compress at contact (awareness). It retains its trajectory and speed across the arc (memory). It reverses its direction in response to the impact (choice). By the working definition above, the ball is alive. Its life is simpler than a human's. It is not a different category.

2.1 · Life Is Primary, Not Emergent

Life does not emerge from matter. Matter is an effect of life.

The conventional story places matter first. Atoms assemble. Molecules form. Complex chemistry develops. Eventually, at sufficient complexity, life and consciousness emerge as byproducts of the arrangement.

This book proposes the opposite. Life and consciousness are the primary layer. Matter, energy, space, and time are what life looks like when it is seen from the outside.

The conventional story is not wrong. It is incomplete. Within a universe where matter already exists, life can be modelled as emerging from it. The model works at the descriptive layer. But the descriptive layer is not the primary layer. The primary layer is the life that is doing the describing.

Principle

Life and consciousness are primary. Matter, energy, space, and time are effects. What life looks like when it examines itself from the outside.

2.2 · The Scale of Life

Existence is infinitely scalable

A cell is a life. An organ is a life made of cells. A body is a life made of organs. A species is a life made of bodies. A biosphere is a life made of species. An enclosure is a life made of biospheres.

This is not a metaphor. It is what the mechanical definition of life produces when applied at every scale. At each magnitude, the whole senses, remembers, and chooses. The complexity grows. The category does not change.

You are a cell in a body that has its own awareness, its own memory, its own choice. The body is not aware of you the way you are aware of yourself. But it registers you. And it regulates what it registers.

A source-level command

A postulate is not a belief. It is not an assumption. It is not an intention. It is more fundamental than any of these.

A postulate is the act of deciding, at the deepest level of a system, what is real, what will be, or what must be. And having that command shape everything that follows.

Postulates are not thought of. They are executed. A postulate is the shape a life takes when it decides something at the level below belief, below assumption, below intention.

Axiom · Postulate

Life generates postulates. Postulates are source-level commands. Everything downstream of a postulate, every perception, every decision, every physical manifestation, is shaped by the postulate that generated it.

The four basics are not moral categories. They are structural positions. A system can move between them. Every posture a life takes toward any other life is legible as one of the four.

3.2 · The Conflict of Postulates

When two postulates conflict, they freeze

Suppose a system holds two postulates at once. X and not-X. Under the two truths of §1.0, this is a logical impossibility. The system cannot process it. It cannot proceed in either direction. Something happens at the boundary between the two postulates.

That something is a freeze. The freeze has four characteristics.

When postulates conflict at full intensity, they freeze. The frozen state has four characteristics: identification, motionlessness, timelessness, mass. This is the mechanical genesis of what a system experiences as weight.

The logical form of the freeze

Truth One says X(1 - X) = 0. The freeze is what happens when a system holds the one thing Truth One forbids.

X(1 - X) = 0 Reason. A thing and its absence share no common class. X(1 - X) = 1 The Insanity Loop. A thing and its absence are identical.

3.3 · Two Depths of Postulate

Not every postulate sits at the same depth

Others are game postulates. They are agreed-upon rules that only hold because enough people, for long enough, agreed to run them.

Where the conflict comes from

When a being is genuinely overwhelmed in a significant game, it takes on the position that was defeating it. It then carries two commands: its original postulate and the adopted opposing one. They cannot both be executed. That is the source of the freeze.

The adopted position does not feel like adoption. It feels like recognition, like having had an illusion removed. That feeling of nativeness is what makes it persistent. If a position feels like "just who I am" and you cannot remember choosing it, it is worth asking whose position it is.

7.0 · Gravity as the Motion of the Rind

Gravity is not a pull

What the body perceives as gravity is the resistance it offers to the rind's inward motion. Or, more precisely, the differential between the body's own internal iteration rate and the rind's motion through the tunnel.

Axiom · Gravity

Gravity is not attraction between masses. Gravity is the felt resistance of a body to the inward motion of the enclosure's rind. What a body feels as weight is the meeting of two motions.

Everything expands. The rate is shared.

Every structure inside the enclosure is expanding at the same rate. Because the rate is shared, relative sizes stay constant. A ruler measured against a table measures the same today as yesterday. Both are larger than they were.

The expansion is not motion through space. It is the motion of space. Or, in the model proposed here, the motion of the rind through the tunnel. The rind is not the boundary of an expanding ball. The rind is the surface of an egg whose interior is being traversed at a shared rate.

Principle

7.2 · Weight as Differential

Weight is the meeting of two expansions

A body stands on the ground. The ground is expanding upward. The body is expanding outward and downward. When the two expansions meet, the ground pushes the body. The push is what the body feels as weight.

Weight = a body's inertia, resisting the rind's inward motion. Not a separate force. A differential between two motions.

A body that slows its own expansion relative to the rind falls out of phase. Inertia lags. The push feels greater. The body feels heavier. A body that speeds its expansion to match the rind drops the differential to zero. Inertia rides with the rind. The body feels lighter without losing mass.

This is the derivation of the two axes treated in the Iteration Codex. The rate axis is the body's iteration speed against the rind. The state axis is the body's structural arrangement against the shell.
