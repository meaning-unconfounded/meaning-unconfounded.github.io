What

A liquidity tree the community owns

People bridge tokens to PulseChain, pair each one with EROS in a PulseX pool, then pair the resulting LP tokens together until one root holds the whole tree. No new contract, no outside service.

Why

The quiet market is the time to build

Bridged tokens arrive with thin liquidity, and usually nobody owns it when buyers return. Building now means the community holds the positions, with EROS as the root: airdropped, no developer fee, live for over two years.

How

Three moves: Bridge · Bond · Branch

Bridge the tokens over the official bridge. Bond each with EROS, equal value both sides. Branch the LP tokens upward, level by level. Start with a 4-coin test to prove it.

You own real liquidity positions across the ecosystem and earn the LP share of swap fees. Every trade through your pools feeds the EROS engine (2% burn, 1% stakers, 1% lock, 1% liquidity). When volume returns, thin pools move first. And you build beside the communities behind each coin.

Level 1 · The idea

Three moves. That is the whole thing.

An ERC-20 token crosses the bridge, meets EROS in a PulseX pool, and the pair becomes an LP token you hold. Repeat, then pair the LP tokens — and a tree grows.

Level 2 · The thesis

The mathematics builds the tracks. Human psychology is the train.

The community’s market thesis — a narrative about behaviour, not a prediction. Read it, then test the mechanics yourself in the lab below.

🛡️

Ownership beats liquidity

Bridged tokens start thin. That is a lack of liquidity, not a lack of ownership. The community owns the coins and the LP tokens. Nobody can take them. Establish ownership during the quiet.

🎟️

Slippage is a tollbooth

A buy into a thin pool moves the price by its size. The buyer pays that impact; LPs earn the swap fee. Green candles attract attention — that is the whole attention engine.

🐻

Bear builds, bull thickens

Thin liquidity at the bottom is simply what a bear market looks like: quiet, cheap, the time to build. When volume returns, the pools thicken from the bottom up.

🪜

Upgrade, don’t sell

Swap token A for token B and your position climbs the tree. Each upgrade moves demand one rung up the stack — so climb in stages, never dump a rung.

🔥

Stored value & the cascade

TITANX burns value; DRAGONX is buy-and-burn; HYDRA leverages both confirmed. The thesis: when the public buys the base and upgrades, demand cascades to the thinnest tip.

🤝

Pump, don’t dump

When someone creates a pool, the group follows and adds. The agreed exit is ~1% of a position a day, so the community is never the sell pressure. A convention — not a guarantee.

Ten coins. Nineteen pools. One root.

Level 4 · The action plan

From zero to root.

Ten short lessons, in order. Each one says what it is, what to do, and how you know you are done. Then take the plan to the build tracker.

1 Verify 2 Bridge 3 Buy EROS 4 4-coin test 5 Base pools 6 Nest 7 Climb 8 Exit

What it is. A PulseX V2 pool holds exactly two assets in equal value. Put both in and you receive an LP token: a receipt for your share of the pool. The LP token is an ordinary token that sits in your wallet, and it can be paired again. That single fact is why a tree is possible.

Do. Work one example on paper: a pool worth $900 plus your $100 ($50 coin + $50 EROS) is worth $1,000, so you hold 10% of the LP supply.

Done when you can say which token you receive after supplying and what share of the pool it represents.

What it is. Four bricks already exist and already run: EROS, the ecosystem coins, the official bridge, and PulseX V2. The tree adds no contract. The only new thing is the arrangement.

Do. Write down your ten coins (the reference list is HYDRA, SHOGUN, S88, JAKEX, LEGENDX, BDX, ORX, PULSAR, TITANX, DRAGONX) with one line on what each one is.

Done when you have a one-page sheet with coin, role and tier (thin tip, community, anchor).

What it is. Fake tokens copy real names, and a wrong address spoils every pool built on it. Some coins carry a transfer tax, which changes what a pool receives: use S88, not SCALE. A coin may not yet exist on PulseChain (SHOGUN is migrating).

Do. For each coin, take the PulseChain address from two official sources, confirm it on PulseScan, and note tax yes or no.

Done when every row has an address, two sources and a tax note.

What it is. The official bridge is already built and in daily use. Staging keeps each step small and checkable.

Do. Hold PLS for gas first. Send one small test, confirm it lands, add the token to your wallet by contract address, then bridge the rest in stages.

Done when the test coin arrived and your balances are on the sheet.

What it is. EROS is the base of every pool, so you need enough to match every coin you pair. EROS trades fire its Flow Fee.

Do. Buy EROS on PulseX in stages and measure from the balance actually in your wallet, not the amount you intended to buy.

Done when your EROS balance covers the 4-coin test, then later all ten pools.

What it is. Prove the mechanics small before you scale: four base pools, one nested pool, one unwind.

Do. Pair EROS with TITANX, DRAGONX, HYDRA and S88 at about $10 a side and copy each LP address. In Add Liquidity, paste two LP addresses into the token search and supply equal value. Then remove that nested pool, and one base pool.

Done when all three work: the screen accepts the LP address, you know how much of each landed, and the unwind returned the pool’s current mix. Do not scale until they do.

What it is. Each coin gets its own EROS pool. Trades there feed the engine, and these LP tokens are the leaves of the tree.

Do. For each coin: approve both tokens, enter equal USD value on both sides, supply, and record the LP address, amount and value. One LP token is worth pool value ÷ LP supply; your position is balance × that.

Done when ten LP tokens are on your sheet.

What it is. Each merge turns two LP tokens into one, so ten leaves need nine merges: 19 pools in all. Pair similar tiers.

Do. Level 2: HYDRA+SHOGUN, S88+JAKEX, LEGENDX+BDX, ORX+PULSAR, plus the TITANX+DRAGONX anchor. Level 3: pair the four non-anchor LPs into two. Level 4: pair those two. Root: the Level 4 LP plus the anchor. Before every supply check amount A × value A = amount B × value B, using values from that moment.

Done when the Final Root LP is in your wallet and every layer is on the sheet.

What it is. ETH → TITANX → DRAGONX → HYDRA → SCALE/S88. Swapping up moves demand one rung up the stack, and the thin pools at the top move the most for a given buy. The buyer pays the price impact; LPs earn the swap fee.

Do. Open the Slippage Lab and compare the same buy in a $1,000 pool and a $100,000 pool. Plan to climb in stages, never all at once.

Done when you can explain why a thin pool moves more and who pays and who earns.

What it is. The tree only means something if it stays. Pump, don’t dump: when someone creates a pool, the group follows and adds. Exit slowly: unwind top-down, then ease out of about 1% of what remains each day, roughly a year to sell about 97%.

Do. Write your exit plan on the sheet now. Each removal returns the pool’s current mix of the two tokens.

Done when your plan is written and the tracker shows the root lit.

Level 5 · The build

You build it. A developer only finishes it.

The whole 19-pool tree can be built by hand on the standard PulseX screens. Every LP token is an ordinary token you can paste in and pair again. No code required to start.

The community · manual

Everything in the tree — no code

  • Verify & bridge — official contract addresses, then coins across in stages.
  • Level 1 — create the 10 EROS/coin pools, equal USD value both sides.
  • Levels 2 → Root — paste each LP address into the token search and pair. In a new pool you set the starting ratio; in an existing pool PulseX fills it in.
  • Hold — every LP token stays in your wallet at every level. Record every LP address.
The developer · optional

The Vault — one ROOT token

A contract where others deposit base LPs and receive one tradable ROOT token. Bundling many people’s deposits and tracking shares is contract work, not a PulseX screen. Your own tree does not need it.

VAULT · developer brief
deposit(baseLP)  // accept approved base LPs

mint / burn      // value LP deposits, mint equivalent ROOT shares
fee routing      // optional · set by the group

Level 7 · Glossary & security

Terms to know. Risks to respect.

Read these before you build — a framework you can verify is a framework you can trust.

LP TOKEN
A receipt a DEX (like PulseX) issues when you deposit two assets into a pool. It proves your ownership share — and it is itself an ordinary token you can pair again.
NESTED LP TREE
This framework’s arrangement: base pools at the bottom, LP tokens paired level by level, one Final Root LP at the top. N coins → 2N−1 pools.
V2 POOL
The classic x·y=k model (Uniswap V2 / PulseX). Holds exactly two assets and issues an LP token. Try the math in the Slippage Lab.
SLIPPAGE
The difference between expected and executed price, caused by limited liquidity. In thin pools it is large — a toll the buyer pays and the LP earns.
IMPERMANENT LOSS
The loss versus holding when the two pooled assets’ prices diverge. It becomes permanent if you withdraw while prices are skewed. Removing liquidity returns the pool’s current mix.
TRANSFER TAX
A fee deducted automatically on buy/sell/transfer (SCALE has one; S88 does not). It changes how much actually arrives in a pool.
THE VAULT (ROOT)
The optional custom contract holding base LPs from many users and issuing one unified ROOT token representing their combined share of the tree. Needs a developer and an audit.
FLOW FEE
EROS’s 5% trade fee: 2% burn, 1% staking, 1% temporary lock, 1% to WPLS/EROS liquidity. Fires where EROS is traded — the base of the tree.
ANCHOR LP
The TITANX/DRAGONX merge held aside at Level 2 and paired directly with the Level-4 LP to form the Final Root.

Security & technical notes

  • Verify contracts. Triple-check addresses from official sources. Scammers create fake tokens with identical names.
  • Smart-contract risk. The vault (if built) requires a professional audit. Interacting with unaudited contracts can result in total loss of funds — and the existing pieces carry their own contract risk.
  • Impermanent loss. Thin liquidity cuts both ways: it amplifies upside in a bull market and amplifies drawdowns and exit difficulty in a bear market.
