Part Thirteen

The chart

Elliott Wave, briefly: Markets often move in five-wave advances and three-wave corrections. A "1–2" is the opening impulse and its first pullback. A nested 1–2 means a smaller 1–2 forming inside a larger one — compression before expansion. "Wave 3 of 3" is the middle of the middle: historically the strongest directional leg when the structure is valid. None of this is a guarantee. It is a map some traders use to organise risk. Read the chart as a thesis, not a promise.

Strip away the cosmology and the macro thesis for a moment and look at what the community is saying about the chart itself, because it's a specific, testable claim.

In technical analysis and Elliott Wave theory, a nested "1-2, 1-2" structure — a micro 1-2 forming inside a larger macro 1-2 — is widely considered one of the highest-conviction setups in trading. The reasoning: Wave 3 is naturally the fastest wave in any standard sequence, and nesting impulses inside each other triggers a "Wave 3 of 3," where multiple degrees of trend align and fire at once. Because lower-degree momentum compounds directly into higher-degree waves, the structure behaves like a tightly wound spring — velocity and volume multiplying rapidly once it releases.

Applied to EROS: a chart that spent roughly two years slowly curving down and back up — a "rounding bottom" locking up market energy — followed by a stair-step 1-2, 1-2 rhythm, followed by a tight handle just under resistance with higher lows, is read by the community as sellers running out of steam right where the setup calls for a breakout.

Read this as a thesis, not a guarantee. Elliott Wave counts are inherently a matter of interpretation, and a "textbook setup" can still fail to play out. This section states the community's own technical argument plainly so it can be evaluated on its merits — it is not a prediction of what the price will do.
