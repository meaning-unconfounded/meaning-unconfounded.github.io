2.1 Extraction is the default

The library's origin story is a graveyard. The Front Door opens by naming the founders of the ecosystem it came from (RainMan, Roody, Mike Dean, Utah, Ally) who "took their fees and their inflation and moved on, leaving tickers still standing where nobody is home." The Newcomer Dossier calls the cost of this model out in its own ledger (fees not taken) because every percentage point a team keeps is a percentage point the holders must recoup from the next buyer.

EROS's answer is subtraction. It is a PulseChain clone-fork of The Grays Currency with the treasury and DAO extraction removed, burning a full 2% per transaction where its parent burned half a percent. There is no founder fee and no developer fee; the founder bought alongside the community. The deployment came from the Jake Sharp Mastermind circle formed before TitanX. These are checkable claims, and the instrument section lists how to check them.

2.2 The narrative half-life

The Eros Corpus states the industry's core problem in one line: "Most tokens die when the chart was the product and the room empties once the chart stops working." The Full 3D Universe book names the same failure from the industry side: crypto has a persistence problem. Marketing is rented attention with a decay curve. When the renting stops, the demand stops, because nothing was ever built underneath.

The Bridge turns this into an equation the whole library reuses: demand is attention plus community plus liquidity. Attention without community is a spike; community without liquidity is a museum; liquidity without attention is a warehouse nobody visits. "Depth without demand is a museum. The Architecture of Participation draws the line precisely: forced participation produces compliance; voluntary participation produces commitment. EROS is engineered for the second and treats the first as a defect.
